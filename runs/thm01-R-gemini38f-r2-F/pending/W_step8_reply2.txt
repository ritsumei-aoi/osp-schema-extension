### 3. Rigorous Analysis of the Koszul Sign & Cohomology

#### 3.1 The Koszul Sign Mechanism
In the extended oscillator algebra, $\kappa$ is an odd central generator ($p(\kappa) = 1$).
When evaluating brackets involving odd generators:
For $X = E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1} = a_1^+ b_1^+$:
$$X^2 = a_1^+ (b_1^+ a_1^+) b_1^+ = a_1^+ (a_1^+ b_1^+ - \mathrm{gb} \cdot \kappa) b_1^+ = - a_1^+ (\mathrm{gb} \cdot \kappa) b_1^+.$$
Because $a_1^+$ has parity 1 and $\kappa$ has parity 1:
$$a_1^+ \cdot \kappa = - \kappa \cdot a_1^+.$$
Pulling $\kappa$ to the left to match the standard schema expansion $[X, Y]_\gamma = [X, Y]_0 + \kappa \cdot \gamma(X, Y)$ yields:
$$- a_1^+ (\kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb}) b_1^+ = + \kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb} \cdot (a_1^+ b_1^+) \implies \gamma(X, X) = + 2 E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}.$$
Without the Koszul sign $(-1)^{\text{fermions to the left}}$, the odd-odd brackets incurred an erroneous relative sign flip of $-1$ relative to the even-odd brackets. This sign flip violated the super-Jacobi 2-cocycle identity $\delta \gamma = 0$, falsely creating an apparent non-zero cohomology class.

With the Koszul sign implemented in `src/C_gamma.py`, $\gamma$ satisfies $\delta \gamma = 0$ identically.
