## Triviality search report

### Criterion and comparison

By `docs/math/C_coboundary_definition.md`, triviality means that there is an
odd linear map \(f\) for which \(\gamma=\delta f\). The comparison is in the
adjoint representation and is **up to scalar**, as specified in
`docs/math/C_inhomogeneous_definition.md`; hence output terms with `Z: "K"`
(the scalar identity) do not obstruct triviality. I compared the exact
rational coefficient formulas in each Layer 3 `C_n_gamma.json` with the
Layer 4 `C_n_coboundary.json`, and checked the listed all-positive
assignments in the corresponding `C_n_evaluated.json`.

For each \(n=1,2,3\), this gives a linear system
\(A_n\phi=\Gamma_n(gb)\), with one column per Layer 4 \(\phi\)-coefficient.
The Layer 3 formulas are linear in the gb parameters. Solving for each gb
coordinate vector separately and verifying the resulting rational equations
exactly gave:

| Algebra | gb coordinates | Layer 3 evaluated \(\gamma\) entries (non-scalar / `K`) | Layer 4 \(\delta f\) terms | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| C(2), \(n=1\) | 4 | 92 / 20 | 212 | Every gb coordinate is a coboundary |
| C(3), \(n=2\) | 8 | 568 / 84 | 2,748 | Every gb coordinate is a coboundary |
| C(4), \(n=3\) | 12 | 1,660 / 180 | 13,976 | Every gb coordinate is a coboundary |

The exact certificates use respectively 6 nonzero \(\phi\)-coefficients per
gb coordinate for \(n=1\), 11 or 12 for \(n=2\), and 16 or 17 for \(n=3\).
Since both \(\Gamma_n\) and \(\delta f\) are linear, coboundaries for all
coordinate directions imply a coboundary for every assignment of the
available gb parameters in each tested rank.

### Concrete evidence and discrepancies

All three evaluated files use `gb_assignment.profile =
uniform_all_positive`, with every gb parameter equal to 1. In C(2),
Layer 3 gives
\[
\gamma(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})
 = 2E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}
\]
(labels `E_eps1_del1_pp`, `E_eps1_del1_pp`, `E_eps1_del1_pp`). Layer 4 gives
the corresponding coefficient
\(4\phi_{H_1\text{ from }E_{\text{eps1\_del1\_pp}}}
-2\phi_{H_2\text{ from }E_{\text{eps1\_del1\_pp}}}\).
An exact all-ones solution has these two coefficients \(8/15\) and \(1/15\),
respectively, yielding \(4(8/15)-2(1/15)=2\). Exact comparisons of every
non-scalar output coefficient likewise have zero discrepancy for C(2), C(3),
and C(4).

There are **no non-trivial gb cases** in these data under the stated
up-to-scalar convention. The apparent raw mismatches are only scalar outputs:
for example, the C(2) Layer 3 entry
`(E_eps1_del1_pp, H_1, K)` has coefficient
`gb_a1_p_b1_p` (1 in the evaluated file), while Layer 4 lists no `K` output.
The analogous scalar entries number 20, 84, and 180 for \(n=1,2,3\).
They are precisely the scalar terms excluded by the comparison convention,
not generator-pair obstructions. Thus there are no non-scalar inconsistent
generator pairs to report.

### Conjecture

For every tested \(C(n+1)\), the necessary and sufficient condition is
vacuous: **the gb parameters may be arbitrary scalars; no equation or
vanishing condition among them is required for triviality up to scalar.**
The exact coordinate checks establish this for \(n=1,2,3\). The general
conjecture for all \(n\geq1\) is that each individual gb-coordinate cocycle
continues to lie in the image of the odd coboundary operator, so every
linear combination does as well. The data support this extrapolation but do
not by themselves prove it for ranks \(n\geq4\).
