3. **Necessary and Sufficient Condition (General Theorem/Conjecture)**:
   The inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is trivial if and only if all $4n$ deformation parameters vanish identically:
   $$ \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in B^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}) \iff \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad (\forall \sigma \in \{a_1^+, a_1^-\}, j \in \{1,\dots,n\}, s \in \{+,-\}) $$

## 4. Rigorous Analysis of Discrepancies & Cohomological Obstructions

To understand the mathematical root cause of why $\gamma \notin B^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$, we examine the cokernel of the coboundary operator, $\operatorname{coker}(\delta) = \left(B^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})\right)^\perp$. Any vector $w$ in the left nullspace $\ker(M^T)$ represents a linear relation that all coboundaries must satisfy:
$$ \sum_{(X, Y, Z)} w_{(X, Y, Z)} \cdot (\delta f)(X, Y)_Z = 0 \quad (\forall f) $$
If $\gamma$ is a coboundary, then the obstruction evaluation $\langle w, \gamma \rangle = \sum_{(X, Y, Z)} w_{(X, Y, Z)} \cdot \gamma(X, Y)_Z$ must vanish for every such $w$.

### Concrete Examples of Non-Vanishing Obstructions in $C(2)$ ($n=1$):

#### Obstruction 1: The Odd Root Self-Bracket Cycle
Consider the odd root vector $E_{\varepsilon - \delta_1}^{--} = a_1^- b_1^-$:
- In the Layer 3 evaluated deformation:
  - $\gamma(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}) = -2 E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}$
  - $\gamma(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, H_1) = H_1$
- Coboundary constraint:
  For any odd linear map $f$, the algebraic identity:
  $$ w_1 \cdot \delta f = (\delta f)(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--})_{E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}} - 2 (\delta f)(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, H_1)_{H_1} + (\delta f)(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, H_1)_{H_2} \equiv 0 $$
  holds identically.
- Evaluation on Layer 3 data:
  $$ \langle w_1, \gamma_{\mathrm{eval}} \rangle = 1 \cdot (-2) - 2 \cdot (1) + 1 \cdot (0) = -4 \ne 0 $$
  This non-zero value ($-4$) proves that the deformation on the odd root self-bracket cannot be absorbed by any linear transformation of generators.

#### Obstruction 2: Root-Cartan Cross Inconsistency
- Generator pair: $(E_{2\delta_1}^+, E_{2\delta_1}^-)$ and $(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, E_{2\delta_1}^-)$.
- In Layer 3:
  - $\gamma(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, E_{2\delta_1}^-)_{E_{2\delta_1}^-} = 2$
  - $\gamma(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--})_{E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}} = -2$
- Coboundary relation evaluates to:
  $$ \langle w_2, \gamma_{\mathrm{eval}} \rangle = -\tfrac{1}{2}(2) + \tfrac{1}{2}(-2) = -2 \ne 0 $$

### Mathematical Mechanism of the Obstruction:
1. **Central vs Adjoint Mismatch**:
   In the oscillator realization, the inhomogeneous deformation originates from the Heisenberg/oscillator commutator:
   $$ [b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \cdot \kappa $$
   where $\kappa$ is a formal central odd nilpotent parameter.
2. **Failure of Inner Absorption**:
   An inner coboundary $\delta f$ corresponds to an infinitesimal automorphism of the Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}$. For an automorphism to absorb the shift $[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] \sim \kappa$, there would need to exist a generator $X \in \mathfrak{g}$ whose adjoint action reproduces the identity shift. However, no element of $C(n+1)$ acts as a scalar central shift on the oscillator algebra.
3. **Appearance of the Central Element $K$**:
   Furthermore, in the oscillator enveloping algebra, the bracket $[X, Y]_\gamma$ produces non-trivial identity operator terms $K$ (e.g. 16 non-zero $K$ terms in $C(2)$, such as $[E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}^{--}, E_{2\delta_1}^+] \to -K$). Since $K$ is central and not in the adjoint representation of simple $C(n+1)$, this deformation fundamentally lies outside the coboundary space.

## 5. General Conjecture and Necessary & Sufficient Conditions

Based on the verified data across $n=1, 2, 3$, we state the general theorem/conjecture:

### **Theorem / Conjecture (Triviality of Inhomogeneous Deformation for $C(n+1)$)**:
Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ for any $n \ge 1$. Let $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation parametrized by the $2 \times 2n$ matrix $\mathrm{gb} = (\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s})$.
1. The $4n$ deformation cocycles $\{\gamma_{\sigma, j, s}\}$ are linearly independent in the 1-cohomology space:
   $$ \dim \left( \operatorname{span}\{\gamma_{\sigma, j, s}\} \pmod{B^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})} \right) = 4n $$
2. The deformation $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is trivial (i.e. $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in B^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$) **if and only if**
   $$ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in \{a_1^+, a_1^-\}, \, j \in \{1,\dots,n\}, \, s \in \{+,-\} $$
3. Consequently, every non-trivial parameter profile $\mathrm{gb} \ne 0$ defines a genuinely non-trivial, non-split Lie superalgebra extension.
