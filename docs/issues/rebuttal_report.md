## 1. Basis Integrity

The Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g} \cong C(n+1)$ is canonically spanned by the quadratic combinations of the standard oscillators. While it is common to define a Cartan-Weyl basis using linear combinations like $H_1 = a_1^+ a_1^- + b_1^+ b_1^-$, any set of linearly independent elements that spans the same vector space constitutes a valid basis for the algebra. 

By defining the basis using the raw quadratic monomials (e.g., $X_0 = a_1^+ a_1^-$, $X_1 = b_1^+ b_1^+$, $X_3 = b_1^+ b_1^-$), we are simply employing an invertible linear transformation of the standard Cartan-Weyl basis. Since the triviality condition $\gamma_{gb}(X, Y) = (\delta f)(X, Y)$ is a linear operator equation over the vector space $\mathfrak{g}$, its validity is independent of the choice of basis. Any linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ expanded over the standard generators can be identically mapped to the monomial basis. Imposing the constraints over the monomials guarantees that the equations span the entire algebra exactly, preserving the structural rigidity without altering the framework of the Lie superalgebra.

## 2. Derivation of Constraints

The condition is not derived from a single bracket, but by eliminating the unknown coefficients of the linear map $f$ (denoted $\phi_{i,j}$) from a subset of these equations.

Consider the evaluation of three specific brackets and equating their 2-cocycles to the coboundary $\delta f(X, Y) = [X, f(Y)]_0 \pm [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0)$:

1. **The bracket $[a_1^+ a_1^-, b_1^+ b_1^+]$ evaluated for the $a_1^- b_1^+$ component:**
   This bracket yields the structural constraint equation:
   $$ -2 G_{0,2} - \phi_{6,1} + 2 \phi_{7,0} = 0 \quad \text{(Eq A)} $$
   (where $\phi_{6,1}$ represents the $a_1^- b_1^+$ component of $f(b_1^+ b_1^+)$ and $\phi_{7,0}$ is the $a_1^- b_1^-$ component of $f(a_1^+ a_1^-)$).

2. **The bracket $[a_1^+ a_1^-, a_1^+ b_1^+]$ evaluated for the $a_1^+ a_1^-$ component:**
   This yields the equation:
   $$ -G_{0,2} - \phi_{0,4} - \phi_{7,0} = 0 \quad \text{(Eq B)} $$
   (where $\phi_{0,4}$ is the $a_1^+ a_1^-$ component of $f(a_1^+ b_1^+)$).

3. **The bracket $[b_1^+ b_1^+, a_1^+ b_1^-]$ evaluated for the $a_1^+ a_1^-$ component:**
   This yields the equation:
   $$ 2 \phi_{0,4} + \phi_{6,1} = 0 \quad \text{(Eq C)} $$

$$ (-2 G_{0,2} - \phi_{6,1} + 2 \phi_{7,0}) + 2(-G_{0,2} - \phi_{0,4} - \phi_{7,0}) + (2 \phi_{0,4} + \phi_{6,1}) = 0 $$
