### Response

**Implementation**
- `src/osc_algebra.py` (extended in I03 design): product with the first-order contraction rule `b a = a b - gb kappa` (kappa moved to the left; sign `(-1)^{deg A1 + t}` from passing fermion letters; kappa^2 = 0).
- `src/C_gamma.py`: for each of the 4n gb parameters (unit vector, gamma is linear) computes `[X,Y]_gamma` for all basis pairs, takes the order-kappa part, decomposes it in `g + C K` exactly; writes `data/C_{1,2,3}_gamma.json`.
- `src/verify_C_gamma.py` (JSON only) and `tests/test_C_gamma.py`.

**gb_matrix**: 2 x 2n, rows `a_1_p, a_1_m`, columns `b_1_p, b_1_m, ..., b_n_p, b_n_m`; entry name `gb_a_1_{sigma}_b_{j}_{s}`; 4n parameters (4, 8, 12).

**Key finding / decision: the constant (K) component.** The order-kappa part of a bracket is not always in `g`: e.g. n=2, `[E_eps1_del1_pp, H_1]` has kappa-part `gb_{p,1,p} (H_1 + 1)`. The constant arises from Weyl ordering and equals the central element kappa*K = kappa. Options considered: (a) project it away (gamma: g x g -> g), (b) store it as an explicit component `Z="K"` (gamma: g x g -> g + C K). **Chosen (b)** because dropping it would silently violate the cocycle identity (the identity was verified including K) and would hide information needed for the triviality analysis; (a) is recoverable by ignoring `Z="K"` entries. Counts of K-entries: n=1: 20, n=2: 84, n=3: 180 of 112/652/1840 entries.

**Ambiguity recorded (parity of gb)**: `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md` says gb has parity 1 although `[b,a]` is odd and kappa is odd (so a scalar gb of parity 1 would make `gb*kappa` even). Assumption: gb are ordinary numerical scalars multiplying the formal odd kappa (kappa written on the left of all words); the "parity 1" is kept as a label (`gb_matrix.parity = 1`) meaning "the combination gb*kappa is the odd quantity". Since only coefficients linear in gb occur, no Grassmann signs among gb arise. The consistency of this reading is supported by: associativity of the product (tested on 300 random monomial triples with random gb), skew-symmetry, and the cocycle identity.
