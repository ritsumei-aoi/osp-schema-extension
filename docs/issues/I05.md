### Response

**Decisions and ambiguity:** Implemented first-order normal ordering with
the literal mixed exchange \(b_j^s a_1^\sigma =
a_1^\sigma b_j^s-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa\), the CAR/CCR relations,
and \(\kappa^2=0\). Gamma rows may target `K` when the exact oscillator
correction contains the scalar identity; `K` is already declared as the
scalar identity in Schema 1 and is not added to its PBW basis. The supplied
inhomogeneous definition says both `gb` and \(\kappa\) are odd, even though
their product on the right-hand side of the mixed exchange is even while
the left-hand side is odd. I considered changing `gb` to even (which would
make that exchange parity-homogeneous) or following the source as written.
I followed the stated parity and literal exchange relation, treating gb
labels as formal first-order coefficients; the inconsistency is documented
in `docs/gamma_schema_specification.md` rather than silently changing the
given convention. This is a source-definition ambiguity, not a failure of
the formal coefficient calculation.

**Results and verification:** Added `src/C_gamma.py`,
`docs/gamma_schema_specification.md`, gamma consistency tests, and
`data/C_1_gamma.json`, `data/C_2_gamma.json`, `data/C_3_gamma.json`. Each
`gb_matrix` has shape \(2\times2n\) and 4n unique parameters. The generated
files contain 60, 334, and 932 nonzero `(X,Y,Z)` gamma rows for n=1,2,3;
10, 42, and 90 of those include the central identity `K`. Tests confirmed
the mixed exchange, CAR and CCR rewrite rules, schema references, and exact
agreement of every undeformed component with the Schema 1 brackets.
`python3 -m unittest discover -s tests` passed all 8 tests, and
`python3 src/verify_C_structure.py` continued to pass all pair/triple checks
for ranks 1–3.
