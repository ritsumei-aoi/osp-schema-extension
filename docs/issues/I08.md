### Response

**Triviality Search Report**

## 1. Methodology

The triviality condition requires solving the linear system $\gamma = \delta f$, which in matrix form is $A \phi = b$, where:
- $A$ is the coboundary matrix (determined by the Lie superalgebra structure alone)
- $\phi$ is the vector of phi parameters of the odd linear map $f$
- $b$ is the evaluated gamma vector (depends on the gb profile)

Solvability is determined by comparing rank($A$) with rank($[A|b]$).

## 2. Case-Specific Evidence

### All-plus profile (gb = +1 for all parameters)

| Algebra | Equations | Phi params | rank(A) | rank([A|b]) | null(A) | Result |
|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|-------------|---------|--------|
| C(2) | 193 | 32 | 32 | 33 | 0 | **NON-TRIVIAL** |
| C(3) | 2,547 | 176 | 176 | 177 | 0 | **NON-TRIVIAL** |
| C(4) | 13,525 | 528 | 528 | 529 | 0 | **NON-TRIVIAL** |

Key observation: In all cases, rank($[A|b]$) = rank($A$) + 1, indicating a **single independent obstruction**.

### Exhaustive sign profile search

- **C(2), n=1**: All $2^4 = 16$ sign profiles tested → **0 trivial**, 16 non-trivial.
- **C(3), n=2**: All $2^8 = 256$ sign profiles tested → **0 trivial**, 256 non-trivial.
- **C(4), n=3**: Representative profiles (all_plus, all_minus, alternating) → all non-trivial.

The deformation is non-trivial for every non-zero gb assignment tested.

## 3. Obstruction Analysis

The coboundary matrix $A$ has **full column rank** (rank = number of phi parameters) in all cases. This means:
1. The null space of $A$ is trivial: the coboundary map $\phi \mapsto \delta f_\phi$ is injective.
2. The image of $A$ is a proper subspace of the equation space.
3. The gamma vector $b$ has a nonzero component in the cokernel of $A$ (null space of $A^T$).

The norm of the null-space projection of $b$ grows with $n$:
- C(2): $\|b_\perp\| = 8.28$
- C(3): $\|b_\perp\| = 18.90$
- C(4): $\|b_\perp\| = 31.56$

This indicates the obstruction is robust and amplifies with rank.

## 4. Root Cause of Non-Triviality

The mathematical reason stems from the structural difference between C(n+1) and B(0,n):

**B(0,n)** uses a supplementary fermion $a_0$ with $a_0^2 = 1/2$. The deformation modifies this to $a_0^2 = 1/2 + \kappa \sum \text{gb} \cdot b_j^s$. This is an internal deformation of the supplementary fermion relation, which can be "undone" by an appropriate change of basis (the coboundary map).

**C(n+1)** uses a standard CAR pair $(a_1^+, a_1^-)$ with $\{a_1^-, a_1^+\} = 1$. The deformation introduces mixed boson-fermion commutators $[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = -\text{gb} \cdot \kappa$ which modify the even subalgebra $\mathfrak{so}(2) \times \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$. The $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ factor (generated by $a_1^+ a_1^-$, contributing to $H_1$) creates brackets that have no counterpart in the coboundary image. Specifically, brackets involving the fermionic number operator $N_f = a_1^+ a_1^-$ and odd generators produce terms that cannot be absorbed by any parity-reversing linear map.

## 5. General Conjecture

**Conjecture**: For $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ with $n \geq 1$, the inhomogeneous deformation defined by the gb matrix is **non-trivial for all non-zero values of the gb parameters**. The obstruction has rank 1 (a single independent obstruction direction exists in the cokernel of the coboundary map) and is intrinsic to the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ component of the even subalgebra.

**Necessary and sufficient condition for triviality**: $\text{gb} = 0$ (i.e., no deformation). The deformation of $C(n+1)$ defines a non-trivial cohomology class in $H^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$ for any non-zero gb parameters.

**Contrast with B(0,n)**: This stands in contrast to $B(0,n) = \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$, where the supplementary fermion structure allows the deformation to be absorbed by a coboundary for appropriate gb parameter values.
