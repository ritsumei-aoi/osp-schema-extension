## 3. Mathematical Analysis of Discrepancies and Root Cause

Why does the inhomogeneous deformation fail to be a coboundary?
1. **Microscopic Origin**:
   The deformation modifies the fundamental exchange relation between the standard CAR fermionic oscillators and CCR bosonic oscillators:
   $$[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = - \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma, b_j^s} \cdot \kappa.$$
   In the associative oscillator algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$, this is a non-trivial deformation of the superspace CCR/CAR algebra by the central element $\kappa$.
2. **Inner Derivations**:
   The kernel of the coboundary operator $\delta: \mathrm{End}_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}) \to C^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{0}}$ has dimension exactly $4n$, which coincides with $\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})$. These are the inner derivations $\mathrm{ad}_V$ for $V \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$. They produce $\delta(\mathrm{ad}_V) = 0$ identically due to the Super Jacobi identity.
3. **Obstruction in Outer Derivations**:
   Any outer odd linear map $f$ creates brackets with even elements of $\mathfrak{g}$ (such as $H_k, E_{2\delta_k}$) that necessarily have non-zero odd projections across even-even pairs $[X, Y]$. However, in the deformation cocycle $\gamma$, $[X, Y]_\gamma = 0$ identically whenever $X, Y \in \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ (since purely bosonic generators involve no fermions and hence no deformed exchange relations).
   Matching $\delta f = 0$ on all $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ requires $f$ to vanish on all bosonic generators, which in turn forces $\delta f$ to vanish everywhere on the odd sector. Hence, no odd linear map $f$ can simultaneously match the non-zero cross-sector cocycle while vanishing on the unperturbed bosonic subalgebra.

## 4. General Conjecture on Triviality

Based on the exact rank results across $n = 1, 2, 3$:

> ### Theorem / Conjecture (Triviality of $C(n+1)$ Inhomogeneous Deformation)
> Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ with bosonic rank $n \ge 1$. Let $\gamma$ be the inhomogeneous deformation parameterized by the $4n$ deformation parameters $\mathrm{gb} = \{\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma, b_j^s} \mid \sigma \in \{+, -\}, j \in \{1, \ldots, n\}, s \in \{+, -\}\}$.
> 
> The deformation $\gamma$ is trivial in the sense of Lie superalgebra cohomology (i.e., $\gamma \in B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{0}}$) if and only if **all $4n$ deformation parameters vanish identically**:
> $$\gamma \text{ is trivial } \iff \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma, b_j^s} = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in \{+, -\},\ j \in \{1, \ldots, n\},\ s \in \{+, -\}.$$
> 
> Consequently, the space of inhomogeneous deformations of $C(n+1)$ has dimension $4n$ and embeds injectively into the second Lie superalgebra cohomology group:
> $$\dim \left( \frac{\mathrm{Span}\{\gamma_{gb}\}}{\mathrm{Span}\{\gamma_{gb}\} \cap B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{0}}} \right) = 4n.$$
> Every non-trivial choice of $gb$ produces an intrinsically non-trivial deformation of $C(n+1)$.
