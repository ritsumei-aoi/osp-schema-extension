### Response

**Triviality Search Report**

#### 1. Methodology

The triviality condition $\gamma = \delta f$ was formulated as a linear system $A \phi = b$, where:
- $A$ is the coboundary coefficient matrix (rows = generator triples $(X,Y,Z)$, columns = $\phi$ parameters).
- $b$ is the evaluated gamma vector for a given gb assignment (one column of $B$ per gb parameter).

The system is solvable iff $\mathrm{rank}(A | b) = \mathrm{rank}(A)$. For general gb, solvability requires $\mathrm{rank}(A | B) = \mathrm{rank}(A)$.

Script: `src/C_triviality.py`.

#### 2. Case-Specific Evidence

**C(2) = osp(2|2), n=1:**
- System: 102 equations, 32 φ parameters, 4 gb parameters.
- $\mathrm{rank}(A) = 28$, $\mathrm{rank}(A|B) = 32$.
- Every individual gb parameter is unsolvable ($\mathrm{rank}(A|b_j) = 29 > 28$).
- Trivial subspace dimension = 0.

**C(3) = osp(2|4), n=2:**

#### 3. Root Cause Analysis

The rank patterns reveal a clear structure:

| n | $\dim(\phi)$ | $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ | Kernel dim | $\mathrm{rank}(A|B) - \mathrm{rank}(A)$ | gb count |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 32 | 28 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 2 | 176 | 168 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| 3 | 528 | 516 | 12 | 12 | 12 |

Key observations:
1. **The coboundary operator $\delta$ has kernel dimension $4n$**, meaning $\dim(\ker \delta) = 4n$. This matches the number of gb parameters, but these are independent degrees of freedom in the $\phi$ space, not in the gb space.
2. **The rank deficit $\mathrm{rank}(A|B) - \mathrm{rank}(A) = 4n$** equals the total number of gb parameters. This means every gb direction independently introduces an obstruction — the gamma columns are linearly independent modulo $\mathrm{col}(A)$.
3. **The trivial subspace is $\{0\}$**: no non-zero linear combination of gb parameters yields a deformation in the image of $\delta$.

The mathematical reason for this complete non-triviality is structural: in $C(n+1)$, the fermionic sector consists of a standard CAR pair $(a_1^+, a_1^-)$ rather than a supplementary fermion $a_0$ with $a_0^2 = 1/2$. The CAR pair produces 4n odd generators (vs. 2n for B(0,n)), doubling the odd sector. Each gb parameter $\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma, b_j^s}$ generates a deformation component that lies in the orthogonal complement of the coboundary image, because the additional $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ factor in the even subalgebra ($\mathfrak{so}(2) \times \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ vs. $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ alone) creates constraints that the coboundary cannot satisfy.

#### 4. General Conjecture

**Conjecture**: The inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is **non-trivial for all non-zero choices of gb parameters**. Specifically:

> For all $n \geq 1$, the second cohomology $H^2(C(n+1), C(n+1))$ (in the relevant parity-shifted sense) contains the full $4n$-dimensional space of inhomogeneous deformations. The coboundary image $B^2 = \mathrm{im}(\delta)$ has trivial intersection with the deformation subspace $\mathrm{span}(\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_{4n})$.

In formula: $\gamma \neq \delta f$ for any odd linear map $f$ and any non-zero gb assignment.

**Contrast with B(0,n)**: This result is in sharp contrast to $B(0,n) = \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$, where the inhomogeneous deformation is known to be trivial (the $2n$ deformation parameters can always be absorbed by a suitable choice of $f$). The presence of the standard fermionic pair — and the resulting $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ factor — is the structural origin of the non-triviality in $C(n+1)$.

**Strength of evidence**: The conjecture is supported by exact rational arithmetic computations for $n = 1, 2, 3$, with a consistent pattern: $\mathrm{rank}(A|B) - \mathrm{rank}(A) = 4n$ in every case. The linear-algebraic obstruction is uniform and does not depend on numerical precision.
