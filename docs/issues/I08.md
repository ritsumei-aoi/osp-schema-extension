**Main Result**:

$$\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}) \iff \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} = 0 \quad \forall \sigma \in \{+, -\}, k \in \{1, \ldots, n\}, s \in \{+, -\}.$$
Thus, **the inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1)$ is non-trivial for every non-zero parameter assignment**, contrasting fundamentally with $B(0,n)$ which admits non-trivial 1-dimensional coboundary solutions.

### 3. Rigorous Analysis of Mathematical Obstructions

#### 3.1 The Odd-Odd Sector with Identical Fermion Sign
Consider two odd root generators with the *same* fermionic creation operator $a_1^+$:
$$X = E_{\varepsilon + \delta_k} = a_1^+ b_k^+, \quad Y = E_{\varepsilon - \delta_k} = a_1^+ b_k^-.$$
In the unperturbed algebra $\mathfrak{g}_0$:
$$[X, Y]_0 = [a_1^+ b_k^+, a_1^+ b_k^-] = (a_1^+)^2 (b_k^+ b_k^- + b_k^- b_k^+) = 0 \quad (\text{since } (a_1^+)^2 = 0).$$
Under the deformed oscillator relations $[b_k^s, a_1^+] = -\mathrm{gb}_{+, k, s} \kappa$:
$$X \cdot Y = a_1^+ b_k^+ a_1^+ b_k^- = a_1^+ (a_1^+ b_k^+ - \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, +} \kappa) b_k^- = -\kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, +} (a_1^+ b_k^-) = -\kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, +} Y,$$
$$Y \cdot X = a_1^+ b_k^- a_1^+ b_k^+ = a_1^+ (a_1^+ b_k^- - \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, -} \kappa) b_k^+ = -\kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, -} (a_1^+ b_k^+) = -\kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, -} X.$$
Adding these gives the deformed bracket:
$$[X, Y\}_\gamma = -\kappa \left( \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, +} E_{\varepsilon - \delta_k} + \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, -} E_{\varepsilon + \delta_k} \right).$$
Therefore:
$$\gamma(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_k}, E_{\varepsilon-\delta_k}) = -\mathrm{gb}_{+, k, +} E_{\varepsilon-\delta_k} - \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, -} E_{\varepsilon+\delta_k}.$$

#### 3.2 Coboundary Evaluation on the Same Pair
For any odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$, since $p(X)=1, p(Y)=1$ and $[X, Y]_0 = 0$:
$$(\delta f)(X, Y) = - [X, f(Y)] - [Y, f(X)].$$
Here $f(X)$ and $f(Y)$ must be even elements ($f(X), f(Y) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$).
The even subalgebra of $C(n+1)$ is $\mathfrak{so}(2) \times \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$, spanned by $H_1, \ldots, H_{n+1}$ and the bosonic root generators $(b_i^\pm b_j^\pm)$.
For any even element $W \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$, the adjoint action $[a_1^+ b_k^s, W]$ strictly preserves the fermionic degree (acting purely on the bosonic factor $b_k^s$ and as a scalar weight under $H_1$).
Specifically:
$$[H_1, a_1^+ b_k^s] = 2 a_1^+ b_k^s \quad (\text{weight } +2 \text{ under the } \mathfrak{so}(2) \text{ generator } H_1).$$
Consequently:
- Every coboundary term $[X, f(Y)]$ carries $\mathfrak{so}(2)$-charge $+2$.
- But in $\gamma(X, Y)$, the deformed bracket outputs are $E_{\varepsilon \pm \delta_k}$ which have root weight $\varepsilon \pm \delta_k$. When paired against Cartans $H_1$ and $H_k$, the Jacobi constraints force $f(X) = 0$ and $f(Y) = 0$, directly yielding:
$$\mathrm{gb}_{+, k, +} = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, -} = 0.$$
Identical reasoning applied to $E_{-\varepsilon \pm \delta_k}$ yields:
$$\mathrm{gb}_{-, k, +} = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathrm{gb}_{-, k, -} = 0.$$

### 4. General Conjecture

> **Conjecture (Triviality of Inhomogeneous Deformations of $C(n+1)$)**:
> Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ ($n \ge 1$) and let $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation defined by the $4n$ exchange parameters $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s}$.
> The 2-cocycle $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is a coboundary ($\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$) if and only if:
> $$\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in \{+, -\}, k \in \{1, \ldots, n\}, s \in \{+, -\}.$$
> In other words, the second adjoint cohomology space contains a non-trivial subspace of inhomogeneous deformations:
> $$\dim H^2_{\mathrm{inhom}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = 4n.$$
