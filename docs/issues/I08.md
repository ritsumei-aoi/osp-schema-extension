### 0. Summary of the result

**The inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1)$ is trivial for every value of the $4n$ parameters gb**,
for n = 1, 2, 3, and the mechanism is n-independent. Concretely, $\gamma = \delta f$ with the explicit
odd map $f = -f_0$ of I07 (shift map), symbolically in gb. The only way to obtain "non-trivial"
cases is to read the triviality condition *strictly* with $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ while keeping
the constant ($K$) components of $\gamma$; under that reading the deformation is trivial **iff
gb = 0**, and the obstruction is entirely confined to the $K$-channel. Both statements are
supported by the data below.

### 2. Independent search for *any* $f$ (Part B) and rigorous analysis of discrepancies

To avoid confirming my own construction, `C_triviality.py` treats all coefficients $\phi_{ZX}$ of an
odd map as unknowns (n=3: 540 unknowns with $K$-rows, 528 without; ≈ 10⁴ equations) and solves
$\delta\phi=\gamma$ exactly, under three readings of the triviality condition:

| reading | $f$ | $\gamma$ compared | result (all n, all non-zero profiles) | nullity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **full** | $\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb{C}K$ | including $K$ | consistent | $4n$ |
| **adjoint** (K acts trivially; "as adjoint representation" in `C_coboundary_definition.md`) | $\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ | $K$ components dropped | consistent | $4n$ |
| **strict** | $\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ | including $K$ | **inconsistent** | $4n$ |

Strict reading — number of failing equations = number of $K$-entries of $\gamma$: 20 / 84 / 180 for
the five "full" profiles at n = 1 / 2 / 3, and 6 / 10 / 14 for `single_a1p_b1p`; every failing
equation has $Z = K$ (verified by `test_strict_reading_fails_exactly_on_K_components`). Examples:

| n | profile | failing (X, Y, Z) | $\gamma$ value | $\delta f$ (strict) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | all_plus | (`E_2del1_m`, `E_eps1_del1_mp`, `K`) | −1 | 0 |
| 1 | single_a1p_b1p | (`E_2del1_p`, `E_eps1_del1_pm`, `K`) | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | generic | (`E_2del1_m`, `E_eps1_del1_mp`, `K`) | 7/3 | 0 |
| 3 | generic | (`E_2del1_m`, `E_eps1_del1_mp`, `K`) | 9/2 | 0 |

Which pairs carry the discrepancy: exactly the (even, odd) and (odd, even) pairs
(Cartan × odd root and $\mathfrak{sp}$-root × odd root): n=1: 6+4+6+4 = 20; n=2: 18+24+18+24 = 84;
n=3: 30+60+30+60 = 180. Symbolically (from `C_{n}_gamma.json`), e.g.
$\gamma_K(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},H_1)=\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$,
$\gamma_K(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},E_{-2\delta_1})=\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}$,
$\gamma_K(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},E_{-\delta_1-\delta_2})=\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,2,-}$.

**Mathematical reason for the discrepancy.** The bracket of $X\in\mathfrak g$ with $f(Y)\in\mathfrak g$
never produces a constant, so in $(\delta f)(X,Y)$ a $K$-component can only come from the term
$-f([X,Y])$, i.e. from the $K$-row of $f$. In the strict reading that row is absent, hence
$(\delta f)_K\equiv0$, while $\gamma_K(X,Y)\neq0$: when the deformed relation
$[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}\,\kappa$ contracts one boson–fermion pair in an (even, odd) product,
the remaining pair is $a_1^+a_1^-$ or $b_k^+b_k^-$, whose expression in the basis of $\mathfrak g$
carries the constant $\pm\tfrac12 K$ ($a_1^+a_1^-=N_a+\tfrac12 K$, $b_k^+b_k^-=N_k-\tfrac12 K$).
Since every one of the $4n$ parameters occurs in some $\gamma_K$ (Part C: 4/4, 8/8, 12/12), the
strict reading is satisfiable only for gb = 0. This obstruction is not a cohomology class in
$H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)$: it disappears as soon as $K$ is treated consistently on both sides
(full reading) or discarded on both sides (adjoint reading, where $K$ acts as 0 in $\mathrm{ad}$).

**Uniqueness of $f$.** In the consistent readings the solution space has dimension $4n = \dim\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$.
I verified that $\delta(\mathrm{ad}\,W)=0$ for every odd basis element $W$ and that the $4n$ maps
$\mathrm{ad}\,W$ are linearly independent; hence $\ker\delta = \{\mathrm{ad}\,W \mid W\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}\}$
(no outer odd derivations), and the trivialising map is unique up to inner odd derivations:
$f = -f_0 + \mathrm{ad}\,W$.

### 3. General conjecture (with proof sketch)

**Conjecture (in fact a theorem modulo routine verification).** For every $n\ge1$ and every
$\mathrm{gb}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2n}$ the inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ of
$C(n+1)$ is trivial: $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = \delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ with
$$
f_{\mathrm{gb}}(X) = -\big[\kappa\text{-coefficient of }\psi_{\mathrm{gb}}(X)\big],\qquad
\psi_{\mathrm{gb}}(b_j^s)=b_j^s+\kappa\big(\mathrm{gb}_{-,j,s}a_1^+ + \mathrm{gb}_{+,j,s}a_1^-\big),\ \psi_{\mathrm{gb}}(a_1^\pm)=a_1^\pm,
$$
$f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ being linear in gb and taking values in $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb{C}K$.
*Necessary and sufficient condition on gb: none* (trivial for all gb) in the full/adjoint readings;
in the strict reading ($f$ without $K$-components, $\gamma$ with them) the condition is gb = 0.

*Proof sketch (n-independent).* $\psi_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is an isomorphism of oscillator superalgebras
$A_0\to A_{\mathrm{gb}}$: $[\psi b_j^s, a_1^\sigma]_{\mathrm{gb}} = -\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa + \kappa\{\mathrm{gb}_{-,j,s}a_1^+ + \mathrm{gb}_{+,j,s}a_1^-, a_1^\sigma\} = 0$,
$[\psi b,\psi b']=[b,b']$ because $\kappa^2=0$, and the CAR are untouched. Extending to the
enveloping algebras and restricting to the quadratic elements gives, for $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$,
$\psi([X,Y]_0)=[\psi X,\psi Y]_{\mathrm{gb}}$, i.e. with $\psi X = X+\kappa f_0(X)$:
$\kappa f_0([X,Y]) = \kappa\gamma(X,Y) + \kappa\big((-1)^{p(X)}[X,f_0(Y)] + [f_0(X),Y]\big)$,
which is $\gamma = -\delta f_0 = \delta(-f_0)$ with the sign convention of
`C_coboundary_definition.md`. The data confirm SIGN = −1 and the identity symbolically for n=1,2,3.
The argument uses only the oscillator relations, so it holds for all n. Contrast with B(0,n):
there the deformation modifies $a_0^2$ itself ($a_0^2\mapsto\tfrac12+\kappa\sum\mathrm{gb}\,b$), and
no such oscillator-shift automorphism is available in general, which is why gb-conditions can arise
in that case; for C(n+1) the standard CAR pair makes the deformation a change of variables.
