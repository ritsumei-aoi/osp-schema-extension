### Response

**Triviality comparison and evidence:** Added `src/search_C_triviality.py` to
compare the evaluated Layer 3 coefficients and symbolic Layer 4 coboundary
image. Every Layer 4 output `Z` is a basis generator; none has `Z = K`.
Layer 3, however, has nonzero `K` coefficients for the uniform \(+1\)
assignment: 10 rows for C(2), 42 for C(3), and 90 for C(4). Since the
trivializing map is \(f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\), the formula for
\(\delta f\) cannot produce the scalar identity \(K\), so each such
coefficient is an uncancellable discrepancy.

For C(2), four explicit independent obstructions are:

| Generator pair \((X,Y)\) | Layer 3 \(\gamma_K(X,Y)\) at all \(gb=+1\) |
|---|---|
| `(H_1, E_eps1_del1_pm)` | `1` from `gb_a1_p_b1_m` |
| `(H_1, E_eps1_del1_mm)` | `1` from `gb_a1_m_b1_m` |
| `(H_2, E_eps1_del1_pp)` | `1/2` from `gb_a1_p_b1_p` |
| `(H_2, E_eps1_del1_mp)` | `1/2` from `gb_a1_m_b1_p` |

For C(3) and C(4), the data supplies an individual single-parameter `K`
witness for each of the 8 and 12 gb entries, respectively; this is checked
by the search script. The same holds for all 4 parameters in C(2). Thus no
nonzero assignment can satisfy the coboundary condition under the Schema 1
convention that `K` is the scalar identity and is not an element of the
Lie-superalgebra basis.

**Case comparison:** At the all-zero gb assignment, every Schema 2 gamma
coefficient vanishes, so choosing \(f=0\) gives \(\gamma=\delta f=0\)
(trivial). At the uniform \(+1\) assignment, the explicit central
coefficients above disagree with every possible \(\delta f\) (nontrivial)
for all three ranks. For each tested rank, independent central witnesses
force all 4n parameters individually to zero; conversely, when all gb
parameters vanish, the deformation is zero. The resulting conjecture is:
\[
\text{The deformation is trivial iff }
\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma,b_j^s}=0
\text{ for every }\sigma,j,s.
\]
This is proved by the single-parameter central witnesses for n=1,2,3 and is
conjectured for every \(n\ge1\); sufficiency follows directly from the
linear dependence of gamma on gb.

**Mathematical caveat:** The source definitions assign odd parity to both
gb and \(\kappa\), making their product even although the mixed
boson–fermion bracket is odd. Also, the computed scalar-identity terms mean
the literal gamma is not valued in the stated \(\mathfrak g\) unless the
enveloping-algebra identity is admitted. The conclusion above is therefore
for the literal formal Schema 2 rewrite rules and the stated Schema 1
convention excluding `K` from \(\mathfrak g\); it does not silently repair
either source inconsistency.
