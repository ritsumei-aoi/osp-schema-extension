## Triviality Search Report — inhomogeneous deformation of C(n+1) = osp(2|2n), n = 1, 2, 3

Script: `src/C_triviality.py` (parts A–E below); machine-readable results:
`docs/triviality_report_data.json`; tests: `tests/test_C_triviality.py` (9 tests, n = 1, 2).
Conventions: `handover/notation.md` §5. Throughout, "trivial" means
$\gamma=\delta f$ for some odd linear $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ (`C_coboundary_definition.md`, "up to scalar");
with the Layer 4 sign convention this reads $\gamma=-\delta f_{\mathrm{can}}$, i.e. $f=-f_{\mathrm{can}}$.

### 0. Summary of the result

**Every** inhomogeneous deformation of C(n+1) of the type defined in `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md`
is trivial, for every value of the $4n$ parameters gb and for $n=1,2,3$, and the data give a proof
mechanism that works for all $n$. **No non-trivial case exists** within this family; the necessary and
sufficient condition on gb is vacuous. I searched actively for a non-trivial case along four
independent routes (A–E) and found none; the reason is structural (§4): every deformation direction
carries non-zero $\mathfrak{so}(2)$-charge, and odd cochains of non-zero charge are always coboundaries.

### 1. Case-specific evidence: Layer 3 vs Layer 4 (part A)

For each $n$ and each of the seven gb profiles of I06, the numeric $\gamma$ of `C_{n}_evaluated_{profile}.json`
was compared entry-by-entry with $\delta f$ of `C_{n}_coboundary_{profile}.json`:

| $n$ | profile | non-zero $\gamma$ entries | non-zero $\delta f$ entries | common support | entries of $\gamma+\delta f\neq0$ | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | all_plus, all_minus, row_sign, column_alternating, generic | 92 | 92 | 92 | 0 | trivial |
| 1 | unit_a1p_b1p, unit_a1m_b1m | 23 | 23 | 23 | 0 | trivial |
| 2 | five full profiles | 568 | 568 | 568 | 0 | trivial |
| 2 | two unit profiles | 67 | 67 | 67 | 0 | trivial |
| 3 | five full profiles | 1660 | 1660 | 1660 | 0 | trivial |
| 3 | two unit profiles | 131 | 131 | 131 | 0 | trivial |

$\gamma+\delta f_{\mathrm{can}}=0$ holds **exactly** (rational arithmetic), with identical supports, in all
21 comparisons. Concrete data ($n=1$, profile `all_plus`, from the two JSON files):

| $(X,Y)$ | $\gamma(X,Y)$ (Layer 3) | $(\delta f)(X,Y)$ (Layer 4) |
|---|---|---|
| $(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})$ | $2E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}$ | $-2E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}$ |
| $(E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1},E_{2\delta_1})$ | $-2H_2$ | $2H_2$ |
| $(H_1,H_2)$ | $E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}+E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}-E_{-\varepsilon+\delta_1}-E_{-\varepsilon-\delta_1}$ | the negative |
| $(E_{2\delta_1},E_{-2\delta_1})$, any bosonic pair | $0$ | $0$ |

Mechanism for the first row: $f_{\mathrm{can}}(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})=H_2+E_{2\delta_1}$, and
$(\delta f)(X,X)=-[X,f(X)\}-[X,f(X)\}-f(\{X,X\})=-2[E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},H_2]-2[E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},E_{2\delta_1}]-0=-2E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}$,
reproducing the $\gamma$ entry that comes from $(a_1^+b_1^+)^2=\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}\kappa\,a_1^+b_1^+$ in the deformed algebra.

### 2. Rigorous analysis of discrepancies (part B — independent solve)

Since A only shows that *one particular* $f$ works, I also solved the linear system
$\delta\phi=-\gamma$ **from scratch**, without using $f_{\mathrm{can}}$: the generic odd map
$f(Z_j)=\sum_i\phi_{ij}Z_i$ (parity-reversing pairs only) and the Layer 1 bracket table give a sparse
rational matrix for $\delta_1:C^1_{\mathrm{odd}}\to C^2_{\mathrm{odd}}$; exact RREF (`SparseEchelon`) was
run with $4n+7$ right-hand sides (the $4n$ unit directions $\gamma^{(\sigma,k,s)}$ and the 7 profiles).

| $n$ | unknowns $\phi_{ij}$ | equations | rank $\delta_1$ | dim ker $\delta_1$ | $\dim\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ | directions consistent | solutions re-verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 32 | 256 | 28 | 4 | 4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| 2 | 176 | 3 416 | 168 | 8 | 8 | 8/8 | 8/8 |
| 3 | 528 | 19 152 | 516 | 12 | 12 | 12/12 | 12/12 |

* **No inconsistent direction and no inconsistent profile** — there is no pair $(X,Y)$ and no
  coefficient $\gamma_{XY}^{Z}$ that fails to be reproduced by a coboundary. Hence the set of
  "non-trivial gb" is empty: because $\gamma$ is linear in gb and every unit direction is a coboundary,
  every linear combination is.
* $\dim\ker\delta_1=4n=\dim\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ in all three cases: the odd cocycles in degree 1 are
  exactly the inner odd derivations $\mathrm{ad}_Z$, $Z\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ (consistent with
  $H^1(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)=0$ for the simple superalgebra C(n+1)). So the trivialising map is
  **unique up to $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak g_{\bar1})$**. (The sparse solver's particular solution, with free
  variables set to 0, turned out to coincide exactly with $f_{\mathrm{can}}$ for every direction — checked for $n=1$ —
  while the homotopy map of §4 differs from it: for direction $\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$ it sends
  $E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}\mapsto -H_1-H_2$ instead of $H_2$, the difference $-H_1-2H_2=\{E_{-\varepsilon-\delta_1},E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}\}$
  being the inner derivation $\mathrm{ad}_{E_{-\varepsilon-\delta_1}}$ evaluated on $E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}$.)
* Part C checked that $\delta_2\circ\delta_1=0$ (the I05 cocycle identity applied to every elementary
  coboundary $\delta E_{ij}$): 32/32 ($n=1$), 176/176 ($n=2$), 24 sampled of 528 ($n=3$), 0 failures —
  so the two operators used here form a genuine complex and the sign conventions of I05/I07 are coherent.

### 3. Does dropping the constant ($K$) terms hide an obstruction? (part D)

In I05/I07 constant terms were dropped from $\gamma$ and from $f$. If $K$ is kept as a basis element the
extra condition for triviality is $\gamma_K(X,Y)=f_K([X,Y\})$ (the $K$-component of $\delta f$ is
$-f_K([X,Y\})$ because $[\,\cdot\,,K]=0$). Checked exactly from the stored `dropped_constant_terms`:

| $n$ | pairs with $\gamma_K\neq0$ | generators with $f_K\neq0$ | $\gamma_K=f_K\circ[\cdot,\cdot\}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | 4 | True |
| 2 | 84 | 8 | True |
| 3 | 180 | 12 | True |

So the deformation is trivial **also in $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$**; the adjoint-representation
convention did not hide anything.

### 4. Why it is trivial: weight analysis and the general conjecture (part E)

**Observation (data).** Each unit direction $\gamma^{(\sigma,k,s)}$ is a cochain of homogeneous weight
$-(\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k)$ in the basis $(\varepsilon,\delta_1,\dots,\delta_n)$ (table in
`triviality_report_data.json`, e.g. $\mathrm{gb}_{+,2,-}\mapsto(-1,0,+1,0)$ for $n=3$). In particular its
$\varepsilon$-component — the eigenvalue of the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ charge
$Q=a_1^+a_1^--\tfrac12=H_1-\sum_{k=2}^{n}H_k+H_{n+1}$ — is $-\sigma=\mp1$, **never 0**.

**Cartan homotopy (verified).** For a cocycle $c$ of weight $\lambda$ and $h$ in the Cartan subalgebra,
$\lambda(h)\,c=\delta(\iota_h c)$ where $\iota_h c=c(h,\cdot)$. I tested this on the data: for every
direction and every $n$, $f(X)=-\gamma^{(\sigma,k,s)}(Q,X)/\lambda(Q)$ with $\lambda(Q)=-\sigma$ satisfies
$\delta f=-\gamma^{(\sigma,k,s)}$ exactly ($12/12$ directions for $n=3$, and likewise with $H_1$ or $H_2$ in
place of $Q$ whenever $\lambda(H_i)\ne0$). Summed over directions this gives the closed form

$$f(X)=\gamma^{(+)}(Q,X)-\gamma^{(-)}(Q,X),\qquad \gamma^{(\pm)}:=\text{the part of }\gamma\text{ proportional to the }a_1^{\pm}\text{-row of the gb matrix},$$

which trivialises the deformation for arbitrary gb.

**Vanishing of the odd cohomology (verified exactly).** Cohomology is concentrated in weight 0, and a
weight-0 **odd** 2-cochain must map a pair of total charge $q$ to an element of charge $q$ *and* of
opposite parity — impossible, since $\mathfrak g_{\bar0}$ has charge 0 and $\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ has charge
$\pm1$. The script enumerated the weight-0 odd 2-cochains and found **0** of them for $n=1,2,3$, hence
$Z^2_{\mathrm{odd},0}=0$ and $H^2(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)_{\mathrm{odd}}=0$. The same charge argument
gives $H^k(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)_{\mathrm{odd}}=0$ for all $k$ and all $n$.

**Conjecture (stated as a theorem for the realisation used here, proved by the argument above and
verified for $n=1,2,3$).**
*For every $n\ge1$ and every $\mathrm{gb}\in\mathbb C^{2\times2n}$, the inhomogeneous deformation
$[b_k^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\kappa$ of $C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is trivial:
$\gamma=\delta f$ with $f=\gamma^{(-)}(Q,\cdot)-\gamma^{(+)}(Q,\cdot)$ (equivalently $f=-f_{\mathrm{can}}$ of I07),
unique up to inner odd derivations $\mathrm{ad}_Z$, $Z\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$. The necessary and sufficient condition
on gb is therefore "no condition". More strongly, every odd first-order deformation of $C(n+1)$ with values
in the adjoint module is trivial, because $H^2(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)_{\mathrm{odd}}=0$.*

Two independent proofs are contained in the data: (i) the oscillator change of variables
$a_1^\sigma\mapsto a_1^\sigma+\kappa S^\sigma$ (I07) removes the deformation inside $U(A)$ — it exists
because $a_1^\pm$ is a *standard pair* whose partner $b_k^{-s}$ supplies $[b_k^s,b_k^{-s}]=\mp1$ to cancel gb;
(ii) the charge/homotopy argument (this section), which is representation-theoretic and does not use the
oscillator realisation at all.

**Contrast with B(0,n)** (why this is not automatic): in `B0n_definition.md` the deformation sits in
$a_0^2\mapsto\tfrac12+\kappa\sum\mathrm{gb}\,b_j^s$, i.e. in the *even* relation of a self-conjugate fermion
without a partner, and the even subalgebra $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ of B(0,n) has no $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ factor, so
neither argument (i) nor (ii) applies there. The extra $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ of C(n+1) is exactly what makes
the C case rigid.

### 5. Where a non-trivial case *could* have appeared, and what was ruled out

* A direction with $\varepsilon$-weight $0$ — none exists: each gb removes one $a_1^\sigma$, shifting charge by $-\sigma$.
* An inconsistency in the $K$-sector — ruled out by part D.
* A sign-convention artefact (e.g. commuting vs supercentral $\kappa$) — ruled out by the cocycle identity
  (I05), $\delta_2\delta_1=0$ (part C) and by the fact that the from-scratch solve (part B) does not use $f_{\mathrm{can}}$.
* Dependence on the Cartan normalisation ($-\tfrac12$ in $H_{n+1}$) — the triviality statement is basis
  independent; the constants only move between $\gamma$ and $\gamma_K$, and both sectors match.

### 6. Assumptions and limitations (recorded per the trust policy)
* gb are scalars (I05 decision); if gb were Grassmann-odd the relation would be parity-inconsistent.
* Only first order in $\kappa$ is meaningful ($\kappa^2=0$); no higher-order obstruction question arises.
* $H^2_{\mathrm{odd}}=0$ was computed exactly only for $n\le3$; for general $n$ it rests on the charge
  argument given above (which I consider a proof, but it has not been independently reviewed).
* Part C for $n=3$ sampled 24 of 528 elementary maps (full check for $n=1,2$).
