### 0. Setting and the two notions of "trivial"

Layer 3 gives $\gamma(X,Y)$ with values in $\mathfrak g \oplus \mathbb C K$ (I05). Layer 4 gives $\delta f$ for the
canonical odd map $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ (I07). Because $K$ is central, $\delta f$ is $\mathfrak g$-valued for
**every** $\mathfrak g$-valued $f$, so the comparison has to be made separately for the $\mathfrak g$-component
and the $K$-component of $\gamma$. This leads to two precise notions:

* **strict triviality** (literal `C_coboundary_definition.md`): there is an odd $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ with $\gamma=\delta f$;
* **extended triviality**: there is an odd $\tilde f = f + \lambda K:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C K$ with
  $\gamma=\delta\tilde f$, where $(\delta(\lambda K))(X,Y) = -\lambda([X,Y])K$. Equivalently, $\gamma$ is a coboundary
  of the (trivially) centrally extended algebra $\tilde{\mathfrak g} = \mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C K$, the algebra that
  actually lives inside $U(A)$, $A = V\oplus\mathbb C K\oplus\mathbb C\kappa$.

### 2. Rigorous analysis of the discrepancies (Requirement 2)

**Which pairs.** The discrepant pairs are exactly the $(X,Y)$ for which $[X,Y]$ has a component along an odd
root vector $E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}$ with $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\neq0$: Cartan × odd pairs $(H_j, E_\alpha)$ with
$\alpha(H_j)\neq0$, even-root × odd-root pairs $(E_\beta, E_\alpha)$ with $\alpha+\beta$ an odd root (e.g.
$(E_{-2\delta_1}, E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})$, $(E_{\delta_1-\delta_2}, E_{\varepsilon+\delta_2})$), and their transposes. All
such $K$-terms are listed in `K_extension` of Layer 4 and coincide with Layer 3's $K$-terms.

**Why (mechanism).** Two independent derivations agree:

1. *Oscillator level.* The deformed relation $[b,a_1]=-\mathrm{gb}\,\kappa$ removes one boson and one fermion from a
   4-letter word $XY$; the two surviving bosons can be in anti-normal order, $b^-b^+ = b^+b^- + 1$, producing a
   scalar. Example (I05): $\gamma(N_1, E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}) = -\mathrm{gb}_{++}(N_1+1) - \mathrm{gb}_{+-}E_{2\delta_1}$,
   and $N_1+1 = (N_1+\tfrac12) + \tfrac12 K \notin \mathfrak g$ because the Cartan of $\mathfrak g$ is spanned by the
   *symmetrized* number operators $N_k+\tfrac12$, $N_a-\tfrac12$ (forced by closure, I01). The constant is intrinsic:
   it does not depend on the Cartan basis, since $\mathfrak g\subset U(A)$ is a fixed subspace.
2. *Cohomological level.* The trivializing map of I07 is $\tilde f = f + \lambda K$ with
   $\lambda(E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}) = \tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}$ and $\lambda|_{\mathfrak g_{\bar0}}=0$, because
   $f(a_1^+b) = \mathrm{gb}\,a_1^+a_1^-$ and $f(a_1^-b)=\mathrm{gb}\,a_1^-a_1^+$ are *not* in $\mathfrak g$ (only
   $a_1^+a_1^- - \tfrac12$ is). Hence $\gamma_K(X,Y) = -\lambda([X,Y])$: the $K$-discrepancy is precisely the
   coboundary of the $K$-component of $\tilde f$, which no $\mathfrak g$-valued $f$ can reproduce.

**Why no other $f$ helps (Part B).** Treating all $\phi_{ij}$ as unknowns ($2\dim\mathfrak g_{\bar0}\dim\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$
= 32 / 176 / 528 unknowns, up to 14 776 equations) and solving $\delta f=\gamma$ by least squares:

Reading: the $\mathfrak g$-rows are always exactly solvable; the $K$-rows contain **no unknown at all** in the
strict setting (every $\delta f$ is $\mathfrak g$-valued), so their residual is simply $\|\gamma_K\|$ and the
system is infeasible whenever $\gamma_K\ne0$. In the extended setting the $K$-rows are solved by $\lambda$.
The nullity $4n = \dim\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ in both settings is the space of odd 1-cocycles $f=\mathrm{ad}(z)$,
$z\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ (inner odd derivations); equal nullities show that $\lambda$ is **uniquely** determined by
$\gamma_K$, consistent with $[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=\mathfrak g$ for the simple algebra $C(n+1)$. The $K$-component
therefore cannot be traded away by any other choice of $f$.

**When does $\gamma_K$ vanish (Part C).** $\gamma_K(X,Y)=-\lambda([X,Y])$ is a linear form in gb. For each of the
$4n$ parameters there is a Cartan–odd pair isolating it:
$\gamma_K(H_1,E_{\pm\varepsilon\pm\delta_1}) = \mp\mathrm{gb}_{\pm,1,\pm}$,
$\gamma_K(H_2, E_{\pm\varepsilon\mp\delta_1}) = \pm\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\pm,1,\mp}$ (sign depends on n),
$\gamma_K(H_1, E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}) = -\tfrac{\sigma}{2}\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}$ for $k\ge2$ (full list in the Part C output below).
Hence $\gamma_K\equiv0 \iff \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}=0$ for all $\sigma,k,s$. Since $\gamma$ is linear in gb and
$\gamma|_{\mathfrak g}=\delta f$ for every gb, this is the complete answer in the strict setting.

### 3. General conjecture (Requirement 3)

Let $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ defined by
$[b_k^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\kappa$.

> **Conjecture (verified for n = 1, 2, 3; general-n argument below).**
> 1. **(Extended / in $\tilde{\mathfrak g}=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$)** $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is trivial for **all** values of the
>    $4n$ parameters: $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = \delta\tilde f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ with the explicit odd map
>    $\tilde f_{\mathrm{gb}}(Z) = -(\text{first-order part of } Z(b+\kappa c, a_1))$,
>    $c_k^s = \mathrm{gb}_{+,k,s}a_1^- + \mathrm{gb}_{-,k,s}a_1^+$; e.g.
>    $\tilde f(E_{2\delta_k}) = -2(\mathrm{gb}_{+,k,+}E_{-\varepsilon+\delta_k}+\mathrm{gb}_{-,k,+}E_{\varepsilon+\delta_k})$ and
>    $\tilde f(E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}) = \sigma\,\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}(a_1^+a_1^- - \tfrac12) + \tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}K$.
>    $\tilde f$ is unique up to inner odd derivations $\mathrm{ad}(z)$, $z\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$. **No condition on gb.**
> 2. **(Strict / $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ as in `C_coboundary_definition.md`)** $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is trivial
>    **if and only if** $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}=0$ for all $\sigma\in\{\pm\}$, $k=1..n$, $s\in\{\pm\}$, i.e. iff the deformation vanishes.
>    The obstruction is the $K$-component $\gamma_K(X,Y) = -\tfrac12\sum_{\sigma,k,s}\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\,[X,Y]^{E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}}$
>    (coefficient of the odd root vector in $[X,Y]$), a nonzero linear form in gb. There is **no intermediate
>    sign or relation condition** on gb (no "row/column balance" of the gb matrix, no sign pattern) that makes a
>    nonzero deformation strictly trivial, because the $4n$ obstruction components are linearly independent.

*General-n argument.* (1) is independent of n: the shift $b\mapsto b+\kappa c$ kills the deformed relations
modulo $\kappa^2$ for every n, and the first-order expansion of the induced homomorphism gives
$\gamma=\delta\tilde f$ with the document's $\delta$ (I07; sign convention confirmed by the code for n=1,2,3). (2): every
$\mathfrak g$-valued $f$ has $\mathfrak g$-valued $\delta f$; $\gamma_K = -\lambda([X,Y])$ with $\lambda(E_\alpha)=\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_\alpha$ on odd roots;
for each odd root $\alpha$ some Cartan $H_j$ has $\alpha(H_j)\ne0$, so $\gamma_K(H_j,E_\alpha) = -\tfrac12\alpha(H_j)\mathrm{gb}_\alpha\ne0$
unless $\mathrm{gb}_\alpha=0$. Both directions therefore hold for all n; the label "conjecture" only reflects that the
computer verification covers n ≤ 3.

*Interpretation.* The deformation is a change of oscillator variables in disguise (hence trivial), but the price is a
central shift: the trivializing map must move odd root vectors by multiples of the identity $K$. If one insists on
the adjoint module $\mathfrak g$ without $K$, this central shift is a genuine obstruction and the deformation is
non-trivial for every $\mathrm{gb}\ne0$. The natural framework for the oscillator construction is
$\tilde{\mathfrak g}=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$ (it is what the extension $A$ in `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md` provides),
and there the deformation is unconditionally trivial.
