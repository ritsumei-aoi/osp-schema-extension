## Triviality Search Report for C(n+1), n = 1, 2, 3

### 0. Summary of the result

For every value of the $4n$ parameters $\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma,b_k^s}$ the inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma$ of $C(n+1)$ is a coboundary, $\gamma = \delta f_{\rm shift}$, where $f_{\rm shift}$ is the explicit odd map of Layer 4 induced by the oscillator shift $b_k^s\mapsto b_k^s - \kappa\sum_\tau \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^{-\tau},b_k^s}a_1^\tau$. The equality holds **exactly** in $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$ (all ordered pairs, all three ranks, all seven profiles and all $4n$ unit parameter vectors). The only subtlety is the scalar ($K$) sector: $f_{\rm shift}$ has $K$-components $\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma b_k^s}$ on the odd generators, and no odd map $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ *without* $K$-components can reproduce $\gamma$ unless gb $=0$. Hence:

* **"up to scalar" / adjoint-representation reading** (the one stated in `C_coboundary_definition.md` §3 and `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md` §2, where $K$ acts trivially): **trivial for all gb** — no condition on gb at all.
* **strict reading** ($f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$, equality in $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$): **trivial iff gb $=0$**; every non-zero profile is non-trivial, and the obstruction is completely explicit (§2).

### 2. Rigorous analysis of the discrepancies

*Which pairs.* $\gamma(X,Y)$ has a $K$-component only for pairs of **opposite parity** (one odd root vector $E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}$ and one even element) — these are the pairs whose κ-part is even and therefore can contain the diagonal monomials $a_1^+a_1^-=J+\tfrac12$ and $b_k^+b_k^-=\tilde N_k-\tfrac12$. The script verified, for every ordered pair and symbolically in gb, the closed form

$$\gamma(X,Y)_K = -\tfrac12\sum_{Z\ \mathrm{odd}} [X,Y\}^{Z}\,\mathrm{gb}_Z,\qquad \mathrm{gb}_{E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}} := \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma,b_k^s},$$

(`K_part_closed_form_verified: true` for n=1,2,3). Examples: $[E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},H_1\} = -2E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}\Rightarrow\gamma_K=\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+b_1^+}$; $[E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},H_2\}=E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}\Rightarrow\gamma_K=-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+b_1^+}$; $[E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1},E_{2\delta_1}\}=2E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}\Rightarrow\gamma_K=-\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+b_1^+}$ — matching the data above.

*Why.* Write $\tilde b_k^s = b_k^s - \kappa\sum_\tau\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^{-\tau}b_k^s}a_1^\tau$. Then $[\tilde b_k^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma b_k^s}\kappa$ while CCR/CAR are unchanged ($\kappa^2=0$, $\kappa$ central), so $\psi:(b,a)\mapsto(\tilde b,a)$ is an algebra homomorphism from the deformed to the undeformed oscillator algebra (with $\kappa$ adjoined). For quadratic $X$, $\psi(X)=X+\kappa f_{\rm shift}(X)$ and a four-line computation (recorded in I07) gives $\gamma(X,Y)=(\delta f_{\rm shift})(X,Y)$ **with exactly the sign conventions of `C_coboundary_definition.md`**. The $K$-components of $f_{\rm shift}$ come from $f_{\rm shift}(a_1^\sigma b_k^s)=\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma b_k^s}\,a_1^\sigma a_1^{-\sigma}=\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma b_k^s}(\sigma J+\tfrac12K)$. Since $[\,\cdot\,,K]=0$, the $K$-part of $\delta f$ is $-f_K([X,Y\})$, which is the closed form above. Dropping the $K$-part of $f$ changes $\delta f$ only in its $K$-coordinate, so $\gamma\equiv\delta\bar f_{\rm shift}\pmod K$ with $\bar f_{\rm shift}:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ — this is the S1 solution. Conversely, if some $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ had $\delta f=\gamma$ exactly, then $c:=f-f_{\rm shift}$ would satisfy $\delta c=0$ with $c_K(Z)=-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_Z$ on odd $Z$; the $K$-coordinate of $\delta c$ is $-c_K([X,Y\})$, and because every odd generator is a bracket ($[H_1,E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}\}=\pm E_{\ldots}$ etc., $[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=\mathfrak g$), $c_K=0$, i.e. gb $=0$. This is the exact reason S2 fails for every non-zero gb, and it is independent of the sign profile — profiles differ only in *how many* $K$-equations are violated, never in whether they are.

*Gauge freedom.* The kernel of $\delta$ on odd maps has dimension exactly $4n = \dim\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ in all settings, and the inner derivations $\mathrm{ad}(W)$, $W$ odd, lie in it (super Jacobi). Hence every odd derivation of $C(n+1)$, $n\le3$, is inner ($H^1(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)_{\bar1}=0$ in these ranks) and the trivialising map is unique up to $f_{\rm shift}+\mathrm{ad}(W)$, $W\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$.

### 3. General conjecture (necessary and sufficient conditions on gb)

**Conjecture (supported for n = 1, 2, 3, every profile and every unit parameter; the proof sketch in §2 is rank-independent).** For $C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ with the inhomogeneous deformation $[b_k^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma b_k^s}\kappa$:

1. *(adjoint / "up to scalar" sense)* the deformation is trivial for **all** $\mathrm{gb}\in\mathbb C^{2\times2n}$: $\gamma=\delta f$ with $f = \bar f_{\rm shift} + \mathrm{ad}(W)$, where
   $\bar f_{\rm shift}(E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_k}) = \sigma\,\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma b_k^s}\,J$ and $\bar f_{\rm shift}(X_{\rm even})$ is the κ-part of $X(\tilde b,a)$ (explicitly $b_i^{s}b_j^{t}\mapsto -\sum_\tau(\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^{-\tau}b_i^{s}}\,a_1^\tau b_j^{t} + \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^{-\tau}b_j^{t}}\,b_i^{s}a_1^\tau)$), and $W\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ arbitrary. There is **no condition** on gb. The class of the inhomogeneous cocycle in $H^2(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)_{\bar1}$ is zero because the deformation is induced by an inner change of oscillator variables.
2. *(strict sense, $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ and equality including the central coordinate)* the deformation is trivial **iff $\mathrm{gb}=0$**; the obstruction is the linear functional $\lambda_{\rm gb}(Z)=\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_Z$ on $\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$, which gives a non-zero class with values in the trivial module $\mathbb CK$ precisely because $\lambda_{\rm gb}$ cannot be written as $c([\cdot,\cdot])$ (as $[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=\mathfrak g$ while $\lambda_{\rm gb}\neq0$ on $\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$).
3. The two senses are reconciled by allowing $f$ to take values in $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$ (setting S3), which is natural since $\mathfrak g\subset\mathcal U(A)$ with $A=V\oplus\mathbb CK\oplus\mathbb C\kappa$: then $\gamma=\delta f_{\rm shift}$ **exactly** for all gb.

Contrast with $B(0,n)$: there the deformation modifies $a_0^2=\tfrac12\mapsto\tfrac12+\kappa\sum\mathrm{gb}\,b$, a relation that cannot be undone by a linear shift of $a_0$ (since $[b,a_0]=0$ and $a_0$ has no conjugate partner), so a gb-dependent answer is to be expected there; for $C(n+1)$ the standard pair $a_1^\pm$ provides exactly the partner needed to absorb the deformation, which is why no condition on gb survives.

### 5. Ambiguities and decisions recorded
* "Up to scalar" in `C_coboundary_definition.md` §3 was interpreted as "modulo the centre $\mathbb CK$" (consistent with "as an adjoint representation"). The alternative reading "$\gamma = c\,\delta f$ for a scalar $c$" was also considered: with $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ it is equivalent to S2 (no solution for gb $\neq0$); with $f$ valued in $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb CK$ it is solved by $c=1$. Both readings are reported above, so the conclusion does not depend on the choice.
* $\gamma$ was kept with its $K$-components (I05) rather than projected to $\mathfrak g$; projecting first would have hidden the S2 obstruction entirely.
