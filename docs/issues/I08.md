### Response

**1. Case-specific Evidence:**
- Developed `src/triviality_solver.py` to map the Layer 4 Coboundary Matrix ($M$, where $M\phi = \delta f$) and the Layer 3 Deformation Matrix ($G$, where $G \cdot gb = \gamma$).
- For $n=1$, $M$ is $121 \times 32$ with a left-nullspace of dimension 93 (representing independent cocycle constraints). For $n=2$, $M$ is $1434 \times 176$ with a nullspace dimension of 1266.
- Applying the left-nullspace of $M$ to $G$ yields the exact reduced constraints: `1 * gb_{a_1_p, b_k_p} = 0`, `1 * gb_{a_1_p, b_k_m} = 0`, etc., for all $4n$ parameters.
- **Trivial Case**: $gb = \vec{0}$. The evaluated schema strictly matches Schema 1, and $f = 0$ perfectly satisfies $\gamma = \delta f = 0$.
- **Non-trivial Case**: Any $gb_i \neq 0$ (e.g. the $gb=1$ profile from `C_n_evaluated_all1.json`). The vector $\gamma$ strictly violates the 93 (or 1266) left-nullspace constraints of the coboundary image, proving it is impossible to express as $\delta f$.

**2. Rigorous Analysis of Discrepancies:**
- The algebraic incompatibility stems from the structural differences between inner derivations and affine shifts. The coboundary operator $\delta f(X, Y) = (-1)^{p(X)}[X, f(Y)] - (-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)}[Y, f(X)] - f([X,Y])$ strictly maps $\mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ through the adjoint action of the un-deformed Lie superalgebra. 
- However, the $\gamma$ terms generated by $[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = -gb_{\sigma, j, s} \kappa$ act as a central-like affine extension embedded within the universal enveloping algebra. Because $C(n+1)$ lacks the necessary derivations to absorb this shift unitarily within its own adjoint representation, the corresponding 2-cocycle $\gamma \in Z^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$ resides strictly outside the exact coboundaries $B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$.

**3. General Conjecture:**
- **Conjecture**: For the Lie superalgebra $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$, the inhomogeneous deformation parametrized by $gb_{\sigma, j, s}$ is **always non-trivial** in the adjoint cohomology $H^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$. The necessary and sufficient condition for the deformation to be trivial (a coboundary) is that the parameter matrix is identically zero: $gb_{\sigma, j, s} = 0$ for all $\sigma, j, s$.
