# Triviality Search Report — C(n+1) = osp(2|2n), n = 1, 2, 3

## 0. Headline result

> **Every inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1)$ is trivial.**
> For $n = 1, 2, 3$ and for **every** point of the $4n$-dimensional gb parameter
> space, $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = \delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ with an explicit odd map
> $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ that is linear in gb. The necessary-and-sufficient condition
> on the gb parameters is **vacuous**: there is no condition.

This is established twice, by independent routes: an analytic construction
(I07, verified symbolically) and a blind exact linear solve over $\mathbb Q(\mathrm{gb})$
(below, §3). The one setting in which a genuine obstruction appears — and it is
a real one — is when $f$ is forced to take values in $\mathfrak g$ rather than in
$\tilde{\mathfrak g} = \mathfrak g \oplus \mathbb C K$; that case is analysed in §4.

Deliverables: `src/C_triviality.py`, `tests/test_C_triviality.py` (16 tests),
`data/C_triviality_report.json`.

## 3. The blind linear solve (no prior knowledge of $f$)

$\gamma(X,Y) = (\delta f)(X,Y)$ was set up as a linear system in the unknowns
$\phi_{ts}$ ($f(Z_s) = \sum_t \phi_{ts} Z_t$, parity-reversing, $t$ ranging over
$\tilde{\mathfrak g}$), with symbolic gb on the right-hand side.

| $n$ | equations | unknowns | rank | solution-space dim | consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 324 | 36 | 32 | 4 | **yes** |
| 2 | 3 800 | 184 | 176 | 8 | **yes** |
| 3 | 20 825 | 540 | 528 | 12 | **yes** |

Consistency holds **identically in the gb symbols** (zero inconsistent rows), so
triviality is a statement about the whole parameter space, not about sample points.

Back-verification: the solver's particular solution (free variables set to 0)
satisfies $\delta f = \gamma$ on all stored pairs, and **coincides exactly with
the analytic $f$ constructed in I07** — two constructions that share no logic
beyond the Schema 1 bracket table.

### 3.1 The solution space is $\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak g_{\bar 1})$

The solution-space dimension is $4, 8, 12 = 4n = \dim\mathfrak g_{\bar 1}$ for
$n = 1, 2, 3$. This is not a coincidence: for odd $W$, the inner map
$\mathrm{ad}(W): X \mapsto [W,X]$ is odd and satisfies $\delta(\mathrm{ad}\,W)=0$.
Verified computationally: $\delta(\mathrm{ad}\,W) = 0$ on every pair for every
odd basis $W$, and $\{\mathrm{ad}\,W\}$ has rank exactly $4n$ (so $\mathrm{ad}$
is injective on $\mathfrak g_{\bar 1}$ — $C(n+1)$ has no odd centre).

$$\boxed{\ \{\,f : \delta f = \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}\,\}
= f_{\mathrm{gb}} + \mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak g_{\bar 1}),\qquad
\dim = 4n .\ }$$

So $f$ is unique up to an inner odd map, and $H^1$ of the relevant complex in
this degree vanishes on the deformation subspace.

### 3.2 Why — the mechanism

The entire deformation lives in one relation,
$[b_k^s, a_1^\sigma] = -\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\kappa$, and it can be undone by a
**redefinition of the fermionic oscillators alone**:

$$a_1^\sigma \longmapsto a_1^\sigma + \kappa\,u_\sigma,
\qquad
u_\sigma = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}b_j^+ + \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}b_j^-\bigr),
\qquad b_k^s \longmapsto b_k^s .$$

Using $[b_k^+,b_k^-] = -1$, $[b_k^-,b_k^+] = +1$:

$$[\,b_k^s,\ a_1^\sigma + \kappa u_\sigma\,] = \kappa[b_k^s, u_\sigma]
= -\,\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\,\kappa .$$

All corrections to the CAR are $O(\kappa^2) = 0$, so this is an **isomorphism**
of the deformed onto the undeformed extension, and $\tilde X = X + \kappa f(X)$
defines exactly the $f$ above. The map $u: \mathbb C^{2}\to V_{\bar 0}$ exists
because the symplectic form on the bosonic sector is **non-degenerate**: the
pairing $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,\cdot,\cdot}\mapsto u_\sigma$ is its inverse. That is
the whole content of the result.

## 4. Rigorous analysis of the one genuine discrepancy

There *is* a setting with a real obstruction, and it is worth stating precisely
because it is the only place where "non-trivial" can legitimately be said.

**Setting.** Take `C_coboundary_definition.md` §3 literally, with $\{Z_i\}$ the
basis of $\mathfrak g$ only, so $f: \mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$.

**Result.**

| $n$ | unknowns | rank | obstructions | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 32 | 28 | 12 | NON-TRIVIAL |
| 2 | 176 | 168 | 40 | NON-TRIVIAL |
| 3 | 528 | 516 | 84 | NON-TRIVIAL |

**Which pairs.** Exactly the $12 / 40 / 84$ triples $(X, Y, K)$ on which
$\gamma$ has a $K$-component. Every one of them has $(p(X), p(Y)) = (1, 0)$ —
$X$ odd, $Y$ even. Complete list for $n=1$ (coefficient of $K$ in $\gamma$,
against $0$ from $\delta f$):

| $X$ | $Y$ | $\gamma_{XYK}$ | $(\delta f)_{XYK}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E_eps1_del1_pp` | `H_eps1` | $\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_pp` | `H_del1` | $\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_pp` | `E_2del1_m` | $\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_pm` | `H_eps1` | $\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_pm` | `H_del1` | $-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_pm` | `E_2del1_p` | $-\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_mp` | `H_eps1` | $-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,+}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_mp` | `H_del1` | $\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,+}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_mp` | `E_2del1_m` | $\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,-}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_mm` | `H_eps1` | $-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,-}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_mm` | `H_del1` | $-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,-}$ | 0 |
| `E_eps1_del1_mm` | `E_2del1_p` | $-\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,+}$ | 0 |

**Mathematical reason.** Three facts combine.

1. $\gamma$ genuinely takes values in $\tilde{\mathfrak g} = \mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C K$.
   The $K$-terms arise from normal-ordering: computing e.g.
   $[a_1^+b_1^+,\, b_1^+b_1^-]$ in the deformed algebra produces
   $\kappa\,\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}\,b_1^-b_1^+ = \kappa\,\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}(b_1^+b_1^- + 1)$,
   and the $+1$ is the $K$-component. It is **not** removable by re-normalizing
   the Cartan: shifting $H_{\delta_1}$ by a constant only moves weight between the
   $H$- and $K$-components of the *same* equation, and the $\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}E_{2\delta_1}$
   companion entries pin the $H$-coefficient. (Checked: the K-components persist
   under both the symmetrized and the unsymmetrized Cartan convention.)
2. Structure constants of $\mathfrak g$ never produce $K$ — verified exhaustively
   in I04. Hence $[X, f(Y)]$ and $[Y, f(X)]$ have no $K$-component for any
   $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$.
3. Therefore $(\delta f)|_K = -f([X,Y])|_K \equiv 0$ when $f$ cannot reach $K$.
   The 12/40/84 equations reduce to "$\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s} = 0$",
   solvable only at $\mathrm{gb} = 0$.

**So in the restricted setting the condition for triviality is
$\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s} = 0$ for all $\sigma, k, s$** — i.e. only the
undeformed case. Note that the *non-central* equations are still all satisfiable
(ranks 28/168/516 with the same solution-space dimension $4n$); the obstruction
is confined entirely to the central component.

**Interpretation.** The discrepancy is a statement about where the deformation
is allowed to live, not about the algebra. $\gamma|_K$ is a scalar-valued
2-cocycle — a central extension datum — and the correct home for $f$ is the
extension $\tilde{\mathfrak g}$ that `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md` builds from
the outset ($A = V\oplus\mathbb CK\oplus\mathbb C\kappa$). Both readings are
reported so the reader can choose; the $\tilde{\mathfrak g}$ reading is adopted
as primary (decision recorded in I07 §2).

## 5. General conjecture

> **Conjecture (triviality of inhomogeneous deformations of $C(n+1)$).**
> Let $\mathfrak g = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ in its oscillator realization,
> extended by the odd central $\kappa$ with $\kappa^2 = 0$, and let
> $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation determined by
> $[b_k^s, a_1^\sigma] = -\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,s}\kappa$ with
> $\mathrm{gb}\in\mathbb C^{4n}$. Then for **every** $n\ge 1$ and **every**
> $\mathrm{gb}$, $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is trivial:
> $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = \delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ with
> $$f_{\mathrm{gb}}(X) = \text{the }\kappa\text{-linear part of }
> X\bigl(a_1^\sigma \to a_1^\sigma + \kappa u_\sigma\bigr),\qquad
> u_\sigma = \sum_{j}\bigl(-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}b_j^+ + \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}b_j^-\bigr),$$
> $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ depending **linearly** on gb and taking values in
> $\tilde{\mathfrak g} = \mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C K$. The solution set is the
> torsor $f_{\mathrm{gb}} + \mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak g_{\bar 1})$ of dimension $4n$.
>
> **Equivalently: the necessary and sufficient condition on the gb parameters is
> the empty condition.** The map $\mathrm{gb}\mapsto[\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}]$ into
> the cohomology is identically zero, so there is no "trivial locus" cutting out
> a proper subvariety of $\mathbb C^{4n}$ — the locus is everything.

**Evidence.** Proved computationally for $n = 1, 2, 3$ as a polynomial identity
in all $4n$ gb symbols (not merely at sample points), by two independent methods.
For general $n$ the argument of §3.2 is uniform in $n$ and uses only the
non-degeneracy of the bosonic symplectic form; it is a proof sketch, which is why
this is stated as a conjecture rather than a theorem.

**Refined form (conditional statement, for the restricted setting).** If one
insists on $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$, then $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is trivial
**iff $\mathrm{gb}= 0$**; the obstruction is the class of the scalar cocycle
$\gamma|_K$, supported on (odd, even) pairs, of rank $4n$ in the gb parameters.

**Structural remark / prediction for the B(0,n) comparison.** The mechanism
requires *two independent* fermionic generators $a_1^\pm$, so that $a_1^\sigma$
can absorb a shift $\kappa u_\sigma$ without disturbing $\{a_1^-,a_1^+\}=1$. In
$B(0,n)$ the fermionic sector is the single supplementary $a_0$ with
$a_0^2 = \tfrac12$, and the deformation is carried *inside* $a_0^2$
(`B0n_definition.md` §3) rather than in a cross-commutator; a shift
$a_0 \mapsto a_0 + \kappa u$ with $u$ bosonic gives
$(a_0+\kappa u)^2 = a_0^2 + \kappa[u, a_0] = a_0^2$ and changes nothing.
We therefore predict that the triviality analysis of $B(0,n)$ is **genuinely
harder** and need not be unconditional — the easiness of $C(n+1)$ is a direct
consequence of replacing the supplementary fermion by a standard CAR pair.
This was **not** verified here (no $B(0,n)$ data is available in this
repository and no external access was used); it is recorded as a prediction.
