### Response

**Decisions and Analysis**:
1. **Investigation Methodology**: I developed `src/check_triviality.py` using `sympy` to formally solve the equation $\gamma(X,Y) = (\delta f)(X,Y)$ by treating the $4n$ deformation parameters $gb_{\sigma, j, s}$ and the $|\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}| \times |\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}| \times 2$ coboundary parameters $\phi_{Z,W}$ as symbolic variables. 
2. **Matrix Reduction & Evidence**: 
   - By constructing the augmented block matrix of the linear system across all generator combinations for $n \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, and taking its Row Echelon Form (RREF), the solver systematically eliminated the coboundary degrees of freedom ($\phi$). 
   - After elimination, the residual rows strictly forced all free $gb$ parameters to zero.
   - For C(2), solving the system isolated exactly 4 independent conditions matching $gb_{\sigma, 1, s} = 0$.
   - For C(3), exactly 8 conditions forced $gb_{\sigma, j, s} = 0$. 
   - For C(4), exactly 12 conditions forced $gb_{\sigma, j, s} = 0$.
3. **Mathematical Reason**: The inhomogeneous deformation modifies the parity-odd brackets via $[b, a] = ab - gb \kappa$. However, any exact coboundary $\delta f$ intrinsically arises from shifting the Lie superalgebra by an odd inner derivation. Because $\kappa$ is a central extension lying entirely outside the inner derivation space of $C(n+1)$, no continuous combination of inner generator shifts (via $\phi$) can perfectly emulate the central cocycle $\gamma$ generated by non-zero $gb$.

**General Conjecture**:
Based on the exact symbolic reduction, **the inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1)$ parameterized by the $gb$ matrix is strictly non-trivial for all $n \ge 1$**. The deformation is trivial if and only if all $4n$ deformation parameters vanish entirely ($gb_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad \forall \sigma, j, s$).
