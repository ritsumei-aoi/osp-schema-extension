### Response

#### Triviality Search Report

##### 1. Methodology

The triviality condition asks: does there exist an odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ such that $\gamma = \delta f$?

This was formulated as a linear system:
- **Variables**: $\phi_{s,t}$ parameters of $f$ (source $s$, target $t$ with opposite parities)
- **Equations**: For each $(X, Y, Z)$ triple, $\gamma(X,Y)^Z = (\delta f)(X,Y)^Z$

The coboundary matrix $M$ and gamma vector $\mathbf{b}$ were constructed from Schema 4 and Schema 2 respectively. Triviality is equivalent to the consistency of $M \phi = \mathbf{b}$.

Additionally, a **K-component obstruction analysis** was performed: the gamma correction may produce terms proportional to $K$ (the central identity element), while the coboundary $\delta f$ can only produce terms in $\mathfrak{g}$ (never $K$).

##### 3. Root Cause of Non-Triviality

The non-triviality has a clean algebraic explanation:

**K-component obstruction**: The deformation $\gamma(X, Y)$ produces terms proportional to the central identity element $K$. These arise from the fact that the deformed oscillator relations $[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = -\mathrm{gb} \cdot \kappa$ introduce corrections that, upon computing brackets of generators, yield constant (scalar) contributions that do not decompose into the Cartan generators alone — the excess is attributed to $K$.

The coboundary $\delta f$ can never produce $K$-terms because:
- $[X, f(Y)]$ maps to $\mathfrak{g}$ (the bracket closes in $\mathfrak{g}$)
- $f([X, Y])$ maps to $\mathfrak{g}$ (by definition of $f$)
- Neither can produce terms outside $\mathfrak{g}$

Since $K \notin \mathfrak{g}$ (it is excluded from the PBW basis), any $\gamma$ with a non-zero $K$-component is automatically a non-trivial 2-cocycle.

**Kernel dimension = 4n**: The coboundary kernel has dimension 4n (= number of odd generators), corresponding to the inner odd derivations $\mathrm{ad}(\xi)$ for $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$. These form exact coboundaries that trivially vanish but do not help absorb the deformation.

##### 4. Contrast with B(0,n)

For $B(0,n) = \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$, the deformation IS trivial (see Aoi 2026). The key difference:
- B(0,n) uses the supplementary fermion $a_0$ with $a_0^2 = 1/2$. The deformation modifies $a_0^2$, which can be absorbed by a change of basis.
- C(n+1) uses a standard fermionic pair $a_1^\pm$ with the CAR relation $\{a_1^-, a_1^+\} = 1$. The deformation modifies the mixed commutators $[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma]$, producing $K$-components that cannot be absorbed.

##### 5. General Conjecture

**Conjecture**: For $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ with $n \geq 1$, the inhomogeneous deformation defined by any non-zero set of gb parameters $\{\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\}$ is **non-trivial**.

More precisely:
1. The gamma columns are fully linearly independent of the coboundary image for all $n = 1, 2, 3$.
2. The obstruction is of **K-type**: the deformation produces non-trivializable $K$-components in the 2-cocycle.
3. There is no non-trivial linear subspace of the gb parameter space on which the deformation becomes trivial.

This conjecture is supported by explicit computation for $n = 1, 2, 3$ and by the structural argument that the K-obstruction persists for all $n$, since the deformed mixed commutator always produces scalar corrections through the Cartan sector.
