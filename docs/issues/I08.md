### 0. Setup and what "trivial" means here
Deformation (I05): $[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa$, $[X,Y]_\gamma=[X,Y]_0+\kappa\gamma(X,Y)$.
Coboundary (I07): $(\delta f)(X,Y)=(-1)^{p(X)}[X,fY]-(-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)}[Y,fX]-f([X,Y])$, $f$ odd.
γ has a central part (`Z = K`, i.e. $\kappa\cdot 1$) on mixed-parity pairs. Because `C_coboundary_definition.md` says
"$\gamma=\delta f$ … up to scalar", I solved $\gamma=\delta f$ exactly over $\mathbb Q$ in three readings:

| mode | equations | f |
|---|---|---|
| `mod_K` | `K` components dropped ("up to scalar" = modulo central terms) — **main reading** | $f:g\to g$ |
| `strict` | all components incl. `K` | $f:g\to g$ ($\phi_K=0$) |
| `strict_cent` | all components incl. `K` | $f:g\to g\oplus\mathbb CK$ ($\phi_K$ free) |

Tool: `src/C_triviality.py` (exact sparse Gaussian elimination with the right-hand side kept **symbolic in the gb**,
so that rows reducing to $0=\ell(\mathrm{gb})$ give the exact linear conditions on gb; then each Layer 3 profile is solved
explicitly and the solution is re-substituted into the Layer 4 JSON entries and compared with the Layer 3 entries).
Equations use pairs $X\le Y$ (super-antisymmetry of both sides was verified in I05/I07). Full output: `data/C_triviality_report.json`.

### 2. Root cause of the discrepancies
1. **Where the `K` part of γ comes from.** For $X$ odd, $Y$ even, $[X,Y]$ in the deformed algebra contains *double* contractions:
   one boson contraction $[b_k^-,b_k^+]=1$ (gives $K$) and one mixed contraction $[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}\,\kappa$. The result is
   $\kappa\cdot\mathrm{const}$, which lies in $\mathbb C\kappa$, not in $\kappa g$. Example (hand-checked in I05):
   $\gamma(H_2,a_1^+b_1^+)=\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}E_{2\delta_1}^+-\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}H_2+\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}K$.
   Such terms only occur for $p(X)\ne p(Y)$ (20/84/180 entries for n=1/2/3).
2. **Why a strict $f:g\to g$ cannot produce them.** $g$ is closed with zero `K` component (verified in I03), so $[X,f(Y)]$ and
   $f([X,Y])$ never contain $K$; with $\phi_K=0$ every `K` equation reads $0=\gamma_K(X,Y)$. Since every gb parameter
   occurs as such a coefficient (e.g. $\gamma_K(E_{-\varepsilon-\delta_1},E_{2\delta_1}^+)=-\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,+}$), the strict reading forces
   $\mathrm{gb}=0$: condition rank $=4n$.
3. **Why this is not a genuine obstruction.** The `K` part is a scalar-valued odd 2-cocycle $c(X,Y)=\gamma_K(X,Y)$. Allowing
   $f$ an even-central component on odd generators (`strict_cent`) kills it: $c=-\phi_K\circ[\cdot,\cdot]$ with
   $\phi_K(E_{\sigma\varepsilon+t\delta_k})=\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,t}$ (checked for all generators, n=1,2,3). This is the
   expected $H^2(g,\mathbb C)=0$ behaviour for the simple algebra $\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$, and is exactly what "up to scalar" in the
   definition removes. So the only discrepancy between Layer 3 and Layer 4 is a central, cohomologically trivial term.
4. **Rank structure.** $\dim\ker\delta = \#\text{unknowns}-\operatorname{rank}=4,8,12=4n=\dim g_{\bar1}$ in every mode: the kernel is
   exactly the inner odd derivations $\mathrm{ad}_\xi$, $\xi\in g_{\bar1}$ (consistent with $H^1(g,g)_{\bar 1}=0$; the I07 unit test
   shows $\delta(\mathrm{ad}_\xi)=0$). Hence the trivializing $f$ is unique up to $\mathrm{ad}(g_{\bar1})$.

### 3. Closed-form trivialization (explains the data for all n)
The deformation is removed at the oscillator level by
$$a_1'^{\sigma}=a_1^\sigma+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^n\big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}\,b_j^+-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}\,b_j^-\big).$$
Checked symbolically (n=1,2,3): $[b_j^s,a_1'^\sigma]=0$, $\{a_1'^-,a_1'^+\}=1$, $(a_1'^\pm)^2=0$ (all $\kappa^2$ terms vanish).
Hence $\psi:X(a,b)\mapsto X(a',b)=X+\kappa F(X)$ intertwines the undeformed and deformed brackets, which gives
$\gamma=\delta f$ with $f=-F$:
- $f(a_1^\sigma b_k^t)=\sum_j\big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}b_j^- - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}b_j^+\big)b_k^t$ re-expanded in $g\oplus\mathbb CK$
  (e.g. n=1: $f(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})=-\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}H_2-\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}E_{2\delta_1}^++\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}K$);
- $f(H_1)=\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,-}E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}-\mathrm{gb}_{-,1,+}E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1}-\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,-}E_{-\varepsilon+\delta_1}+\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}E_{-\varepsilon-\delta_1}$
  (n=1; in general the $a_1^+a_1^-$ part of $H_1$ produces $\sum_j$ of such terms);
- $f=0$ on all other even generators (they contain no fermion).
For n=1,2,3 the symbolic $\delta f$ of this map equals the symbolic Schema 2 γ **entry by entry, including the `K` parts**
(0 mismatches), independently of the linear solve.

### 4. Conjecture (general rule on gb)
> **Conjecture / Proposition.** For every $n\ge1$ and every $\mathrm{gb}\in\mathbb C^{4n}$ the inhomogeneous deformation
> $[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa$ of $C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is **trivial**: $\gamma=\delta f$ for the odd map $f$ of §3,
> modulo central terms (and exactly, if $f$ may take a central value $\phi_K(E_{\sigma\varepsilon+t\delta_k})=\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,t}$).
> The necessary and sufficient condition on gb is therefore **empty** (all gb). Only under the strict reading
> "$f:g\to g$ and the $\kappa\cdot1$ term must match" is the condition $\mathrm{gb}=0$, and that obstruction is purely the central,
> cohomologically trivial $K$-component.

Evidence: exact computation for n=1,2,3 on all 8 profiles and symbolically on all of $\mathbb C^{4n}$; the oscillator argument in §3 is
independent of $n$ and constitutes a proof sketch for general $n$ (it uses only that $\kappa$ is odd central with $\kappa^2=0$ and that
$[b_j^-,b_j^+]=1$ is invertible, so every mixed pairing $(b|a)$ can be absorbed into a shift of $a$).
Contrast with B(0,n) (not computed here): there the deformation enters through $a_0^2$, a single self-paired odd generator,
so the same shift mechanism is not obviously available — that is a plausible reason why the C-type answer can differ from the B-type one.
