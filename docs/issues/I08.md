# Triviality Search Report: Inhomogeneous Deformations of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$

### 1. Executive Summary & Main Theorem
Through exact linear algebraic and cohomological analysis implemented in `src/triviality_analysis.py`, we investigated the solvability of the coboundary condition:
$$\gamma(X, Y) = (\delta f)(X, Y) \quad \forall X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$$
where $\gamma$ is the inhomogeneous 2-cocycle parameterized by $4n$ deformation parameters $\mathrm{gb} \in \mathbb{R}^{4n}$, and $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ is an odd linear map parameterized by $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}, \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}, \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})$.

**Core Finding**:
Across all tested ranks ($n=1, 2, 3$), the linear space of deformation cocycles $\operatorname{im}(G)$ is **completely transverse** to the coboundary subspace $\operatorname{im}(M)$:
$$\operatorname{im}(G) \cap \operatorname{im}(M) = \{0\}.$$
Consequently, the inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is **strictly non-trivial** for ANY non-zero parameter assignment. It is trivial **if and only if $\mathrm{gb} = 0$**.

### 3. Rigorous Analysis of Discrepancies & Mathematical Mechanism

Why is the inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ non-trivial, whereas $B(0,n) = \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$ was trivial?

#### 3.1 Structural Contrast between $B(0,n)$ and $C(n+1)$
- In $B(0,n) = \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$, the odd sector is generated by a **single supplementary fermion** $a_0$ with $a_0^2 = 1/2$. The deformation modifies $a_0^2 \mapsto 1/2 + \kappa \sum \mathrm{gb}_j b_j$. This modification is equivalent to an internal linear shift $a_0 \mapsto a_0 + \kappa \sum c_j b_j$, which represents an odd inner/elementary automorphism of the oscillator superalgebra. Hence the deformation cocycle is a coboundary.
- In $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$, the fermionic sector consists of a **canonical CAR pair** $(a_1^+, a_1^-)$ with $\{a_1^-, a_1^+\} = 1$. The even subalgebra is strictly enlarged to $\mathfrak{so}(2) \times \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ with an $(n+1)$-dimensional Cartan subalgebra containing $H_1 = a_1^+ a_1^- + b_1^+ b_1^-$.

#### 3.2 The Fundamental Obstruction at the Cartan-Root Interface
Consider $C(2)$ ($n=1$):
In the undeformed algebra, $[H_1, H_2] = 0$.
Under the coboundary operator, because $H_1, H_2 \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$ and $[H_1, H_2] = 0$:
$$(\delta f)(H_1, H_2) = [H_1, f(H_2)] - [H_2, f(H_1)].$$
Because $f(H_1), f(H_2) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$, they expand in the odd root basis $\{E_{\pm \varepsilon \pm \delta_1}\}$. Since root vectors are simultaneous eigenvectors of $\operatorname{ad}_{H_1}$ and $\operatorname{ad}_{H_2}$, the action of $[H_i, \cdot]$ scales each root vector by its root weight.
Meanwhile, under the deformation:
$$\gamma(H_1, H_2) = \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+, b_1^+} E_{-\varepsilon - \delta_1} - \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+, b_1^-} E_{-\varepsilon + \delta_1} - \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^-, b_1^+} E_{\varepsilon - \delta_1} + \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^-, b_1^-} E_{\varepsilon + \delta_1}.$$
At the same time, the deformation acts on mixed brackets such as $[H_1, E_{\varepsilon + \delta_1}]$:
$$\gamma(H_1, E_{\varepsilon + \delta_1}) = \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+, b_1^+} (H_1 + 2H_2) - \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+, b_1^-} E_{2\delta_1\_p}.$$
Solving $(\delta f)(X, Y) = \gamma(X, Y)$ requires $f$ to simultaneously satisfy:
1. Matching the odd components produced in $[H_1, H_2]_\gamma$ (which fixes the projections of $f(H_1)$ and $f(H_2)$).
2. Matching the even components produced in $[H_i, E_{\pm \varepsilon \pm \delta_1}]_\gamma$ (which imposes contradictory values on $f(E_{\pm \varepsilon \pm \delta_1})$).

Left nullvector projection of $M^T$ yields exact scalar equations:
- $1 \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+, b_1^+} = 0$
- $1 \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+, b_1^-} = 0$
- $1 \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^-, b_1^+} = 0$
- $1 \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{a_1^-, b_1^-} = 0$

No cancellation between different $\mathrm{gb}$ parameters can resolve these obstructions because the parameters modulate distinct root-grading sectors under the $\mathfrak{so}(2) \times \mathfrak{h}_{\mathfrak{sp}}$ weight decomposition.

### 4. General Mathematical Conjecture

**Conjecture (Triviality of Inhomogeneous Deformations for $C(n+1)$)**:
Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ with $n \ge 1$, realized via the CAR pair $(a_1^+, a_1^-)$ and CCR bosons $(b_j^+, b_j^-)_{j=1}^n$. Let $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation 2-cocycle determined by the exchange relations:
$$[b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = -\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma, b_j^s} \cdot \kappa \quad (\sigma \in \{+,-\}, s \in \{+,-\}, j = 1, \dots, n).$$
Then:
1. The deformation $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is a coboundary ($\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in B^2_{\text{odd}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$) **if and only if** all $4n$ deformation parameters vanish:
   $$\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^\sigma, b_j^s} = 0 \quad \forall \sigma \in \{+,-\}, j \in \{1,\dots,n\}, s \in \{+,-\}.$$
2. The second adjoint cohomology group has an odd subspace containing the deformation classes:
   $$\dim \operatorname{Span}\{ [\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}] \mid \mathrm{gb} \in \mathbb{R}^{4n} \} = 4n.$$
   In particular, the inhomogeneous deformation gives a non-trivial $4n$-dimensional family of deformations of $C(n+1)$.
