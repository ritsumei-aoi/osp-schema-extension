**Method** (`src/C_triviality.py`, exact rational arithmetic, no floating point):
- $\gamma(\mathrm{gb})$ is linear in gb (Schema 2). $\delta(\phi)$ is linear in $\phi$ (Schema 4). Triviality is $\gamma(\mathrm{gb})\in\operatorname{im}\delta$. Since the image is a subspace, the scalar in "up to scalar" does not matter.
- Gaussian elimination on $[D\mid\Gamma]$ over the components $(X,Y,Z)$, with full reduction. Rows left with no $\phi$-part give linear conditions on gb. These are the obstructions. Each reduced row carries the set of $(X,Y,Z)$ rows it came from, which gives generator-pair provenance (an upper bound on the true support).

**Layer 3 vs Layer 4, all-+1 profile, $n=1$ (case-specific evidence)**:
- Non-central part ($Z\neq K$): I solved $\delta(\phi)=\gamma_{\rm nonK}$ exactly with sympy, over 216 equations. A solution exists with zero residual. The family has 4 free parameters (`phi_6_1`, `phi_6_3`, `phi_7_1`, `phi_7_3`). Example: $\phi_{4,0} = 1-\phi_{7,1}$, $\phi_{4,2} = \phi_{7,3}-1$, $\phi_{0,6} = -\phi_{6,3}-1$. So for all-+1, the non-central structure **coincides** with a coboundary.
- Central part ($Z=K$): this is where the structures **do not** coincide. $\gamma$ has $K$-components, e.g. $\gamma(W(a_1^+,b_1^-),\,W(a_1^+,a_1^-))^K=\tfrac12\,\mathrm{gb}_{a_1^+,b_1^+}$ type terms, and $\delta f$ never has a $K$-component because $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$.

- Strict obstructions: for every $n$, each single gb parameter is an obstruction. The forms are $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}=0$, one for each of the $4n$ parameters. All of them are supported only on $K$-rows (checked for $n=1$: every supporting row has $Z=K$).

- Profiles: `all_plus` and `alt` are **not** trivial under the strict definition. Both are trivial when $K$ is removed.

**Variant (investigation, not the strict definition)**: allowing $f(Z_j)=\psi_j K$ for odd $Z_j$ (consistent with parity, since $K$ is even and central). Then every gb is trivial. The explicit witness for `all_plus`, $n=1$, is $\psi_j = \tfrac12$ for all four odd basis elements. This solves all 24 $K$-equations exactly.

**Conjecture (based on the data for $n=1,2,3$)**:
1. The non-central part of $\gamma(\mathrm{gb})$ lies in the image of $\delta$ for **every** gb.
2. Under the stated definition ($f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$), $\gamma(\mathrm{gb})$ is a coboundary **if and only if $\mathrm{gb}=0$**. Equivalently, the trivial locus is $\{0\}\subset\mathbb C^{4n}$. The only obstructions are the $4n$ equations $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}=0$, which come from the central $K$-component.
3. If $K$-valued $f$ is allowed, every gb is trivial.

**Why the $K$-component appears (mathematical reason)**: the Weyl-symmetric quadratics are not closed under the deformed product. A single boson–fermion contraction $[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}\,\kappa$ inside a degree-4 word leaves a degree-2 $\kappa$-term (this is the non-central part). Reordering the remaining degree-2 part produces a $\kappa\cdot 1$ term, which is $\kappa K$. The $K$-part of $[X,Y]$ is canonical: changing the lift of $X$ to $X+c\cdot 1$ does not change the bracket, because constants are central. So the obstruction is not an artifact of the choice of basis lift.

**Limitations**:
- The conjecture is verified for $n=1,2,3$ only. I did not prove a general-$n$ formula.
- For $n=3$ the cocycle check on the coboundary (I07) was probabilistic (seeded random combination). The triviality analysis itself is exact.
- The result depends on the convention that $f$ maps into $\mathfrak g$ (strict) and not into $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C K$. I report both, and the strict one is the one stated in `docs/math/C_coboundary_definition.md`.
