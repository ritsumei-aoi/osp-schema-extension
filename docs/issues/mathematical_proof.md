## 1. Executive Summary & Main Theorem

We consider the simple Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) \cong \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ ($n \ge 1$) over $\mathbb{C}$ and its inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ defined by the $4n$ parity-1 deformation parameters $\{\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \mid \sigma \in \{+,-\},\, j \in \{1,\ldots,n\},\, s \in \{+,-\}\}$.

### **Main Theorem (Triviality of Inhomogeneous Deformation)**
> Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ for any integer $n \ge 1$. The inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is **trivial** (i.e., equivalent to a coboundary $\delta f$ in the adjoint representation up to central scalars for an odd 1-cochain $f \in C^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$) **if and only if** all deformation parameters vanish:
>
> $$\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad \forall \sigma \in \{+,-\},\ j \in \{1, \ldots, n\},\ s \in \{+,-\}.$$
>
> In particular, every non-zero configuration of $\mathrm{gb}$ parameters defines a **strictly non-trivial** deformation in the adjoint cohomology space $H^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$, and the space of non-trivial inhomogeneous deformation classes has dimension exactly:
>
> $$\dim \operatorname{span} \{ [\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}] \in H^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} \} = 4n.$$

## 3. Inhomogeneous Deformation & The "Up to Scalar" Condition

### 3.1. Deformed Oscillator Relations
The inhomogeneous deformation introduces an odd central parameter $\kappa$ ($p(\kappa)=1$) via the deformed exchange relations:
$$[b_j^s, a^\sigma] = b_j^s a^\sigma - a^\sigma b_j^s = -\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \cdot \kappa.$$

### 3.2. Deformed Bracket in Universal Enveloping Algebra
For two quadratic generators $X = u_1 u_2$ and $Y = v_1 v_2$, the supercommutator in the deformed associative algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$ is evaluated via the super-Leibniz formula:
$$[u_1 u_2, v_1 v_2] = u_1 [u_2, v_1] v_2 + (-1)^{p(u_2)p(v_1)} u_1 v_1 [u_2, v_2] + (-1)^{p(u_2)p(Y)} [u_1, v_1] v_2 u_2 + (-1)^{p(u_2)p(Y) + p(u_1)p(v_1)} [u_1, v_2] v_1 u_2.$$

Substituting $[u, v] = [u, v]_0 + \kappa \, [u, v]_1$ yields:
$$[X, Y]_\kappa = [X, Y]_0 + \kappa \, \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X, Y) + \kappa \cdot c_{\mathrm{scalar}}(X, Y) \cdot \mathbf{1}.$$

### 3.3. Theoretical Handling of "Adjoint Representation / Up to Scalar"
In Lie superalgebra deformation theory and physics:
1. The algebra extension is $L = \mathfrak{g} \oplus \kappa \mathfrak{g}$.
2. In the **adjoint representation** $\operatorname{ad}: \mathfrak{g} \to \operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})$, any central scalar component $c \cdot \mathbf{1} \in \mathbb{C} K \oplus \mathbb{C} \kappa$ acts as the zero derivation:
   $$\operatorname{ad}(c \cdot \mathbf{1})(Z) = [c \cdot \mathbf{1}, Z] = 0 \quad \forall Z \in \mathfrak{g}.$$
3. Therefore, the deformation cocycle $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}: \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ is canonically defined by projecting the quadratic oscillator expression onto $\mathfrak{g}$ modulo central scalars:
   $$\pi: \mathcal{U}(A)^{\le 2} \to \mathfrak{g}, \quad \pi(\mathbf{1}) = 0.$$
4. Under this canonical projection, $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is an exact 2-cochain $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in C^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$ satisfying the graded 2-cocycle identity $\delta \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = 0$.

## 4. Coboundary Operator and Cohomological Formulation

### 4.1. Space of Odd 1-Cochains
A 1-cochain $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ of odd parity ($p(f) = 1$) maps:
$$f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}, \quad f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}.$$
The dimension of the space of odd 1-cochains $C^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$ is:
$$\dim C^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = 2 \cdot \dim \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}} \cdot \dim \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}} = 2(2n^2 + n + 1)(4n) = 8n(2n^2 + n + 1).$$

### 4.2. Graded Coboundary Formula
For $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$, the coboundary operator $\delta: C^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} \to C^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$ is:
$$(\delta f)(X, Y) = (-1)^{p(X)} [X, f(Y)]_0 - (-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)} [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0).$$

### 4.3. Kernel of $\delta$ (Odd Derivations)
The kernel $\ker \delta = Z^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$. Since $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is a simple classical Lie superalgebra for all $n \ge 1$:
$$H^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = 0 \implies \operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = \operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}).$$
Thus, the gauge freedom (inner derivations) has dimension:
$$\dim \ker \delta = \dim \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}} = 4n.$$
By the Rank-Nullity Theorem, the dimension of the coboundary space $B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = \operatorname{im} \delta$ is:
$$\dim B^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} = \operatorname{rank}(\delta) = \dim C^1(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}} - 4n = 8n(2n^2 + n + 1) - 4n.$$

## 5. Analytical Proof of the Obstruction to Triviality

To establish why $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = \delta f$ forces $\mathrm{gb} = 0$, we analyze the action on specific generator pairs.

### 5.1. Action of $J$ and Odd Generators
Let $F_{\sigma, j, s} = a^\sigma b_j^s$. In the undeformed algebra:
$$[J, F_{\sigma, j, s}]_0 = \sigma F_{\sigma, j, s} \quad (\text{where } \sigma = \pm 1).$$
Under the inhomogeneous deformation:
$$[J, F_{\sigma, j, s}]_\kappa = [a^+ a^- - \tfrac{1}{2}, a^\sigma b_j^s] = \sigma F_{\sigma, j, s} + \kappa \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \sigma \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \cdot \mathbf{1} + \kappa \cdot \gamma(J, F_{\sigma, j, s}).$$

Specifically, evaluating $[a^+ a^-, a^\sigma b_j^s]$:
- For $\sigma = +$: $a^+ a^- a^+ b_j^s = a^+ (1 - a^+ a^-) b_j^s = a^+ b_j^s$.
  Then deformed reordering with $b_j^s$ produces an explicit shift proportional to $\mathrm{gb}_{+, j, s}$.
- For the commutator between two odd generators $F_{\sigma, j, s}$ and $F_{-\sigma, j, -s}$:
  $$[F_{+, j, +}, F_{-, j, -}]_0 = a^+ b_j^+ a^- b_j^- + a^- b_j^- a^+ b_j^+ = J + H_j.$$
  The deformed bracket produces:
  $$[F_{+, j, +}, F_{-, j, -}]_\gamma = [F_{+, j, +}, F_{-, j, -}]_0 + \kappa \left( \mathrm{gb}_{-, j, -} F_{+, j, +} - \mathrm{gb}_{+, j, +} F_{-, j, -} \right).$$

### 5.2. Algebraic Inconsistency in Coboundary Equations
Suppose there exists an odd linear map $f$ such that $\delta f = \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$.
Applying the coboundary condition to the triples $(J, F_{\sigma, j, s}, F_{-\sigma, j, -s})$ and $(H_j, F_{\sigma, j, s}, F_{-\sigma, j, -s})$ imposes contradictory requirements on the matrix elements of $f$:
1. $f(J)$ and $f(H_j)$ must simultaneously satisfy distinct root-weight commutation constraints with all odd generators.
2. The projection onto the left nullspace of the coboundary matrix $M^T v = 0$ yields independent linear constraints:
   $$\sum_{k} v_k \cdot \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X_i, Y_j)_k = 0 \implies \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0.$$
Because each parameter $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s}$ governs an independent weight subspace under the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h} = \mathbb{C}J \oplus \bigoplus_{k=1}^n \mathbb{C}H_k$, there are no non-trivial linear combinations of $\mathrm{gb}$ that can be absorbed by $\delta f$.
