## 0. Main result

> **Theorem.** Let $n\ge 1$ and let $\mathrm{gb}=(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s})\in\mathbf C^{4n}$ be arbitrary. The inhomogeneous deformation cocycle $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ of $\mathfrak g=C(n+1)$ is a coboundary:
> $$\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$$
> for the explicit odd linear map $f_{\mathrm{gb}}:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ given in §3.3. Consequently the deformation is **trivial for every choice of $\mathrm{gb}$**: the necessary and sufficient condition on $\mathrm{gb}$ for triviality is *no condition at all*.
>
> Moreover (i) $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}\neq 0$ whenever $\mathrm{gb}\neq 0$ (the map $\mathrm{gb}\mapsto\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is injective), so every non-zero $\mathrm{gb}$ gives a non-zero but cohomologically trivial deformation; (ii) the map $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is unique up to adding an inner odd derivation $\operatorname{ad}Y$, $Y\in\mathfrak g_{\bar 1}$; (iii) more generally $H^p(\mathfrak g;\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$ for all $p\ge 0$ — *every* odd deformation of $C(n+1)$ with values in the adjoint module is trivial, for every $n$.

Two independent proofs are given: a **constructive** one (§3; it produces $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ and explains the "adjoint representation / up to scalar" clause) and a **cohomological** one (§4; it shows the relevant cohomology group is zero). Both are valid for all $n\ge 1$; both are verified computationally for $n=1,2,3$ with exact rational arithmetic and symbolic $\mathrm{gb}$ (§6).

## 1. Set-up and conventions

### 1.1 The deformed oscillator algebra
Let $W$ be the Weyl–Clifford algebra with generators $a^{\pm}$ (odd) and $b_k^{\pm}$, $k=1,\dots,n$ (even), relations
$$\{a^-,a^+\}=1,\quad (a^\pm)^2=0,\quad [b_k^-,b_l^+]=\delta_{kl},\quad [b_k^\pm,b_l^\pm]=0,\quad [b_k^s,a^\sigma]=0 .$$
(This is $\mathcal U(A)/(K-1)$ in the notation of `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md`.)
The deformed algebra $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is generated by the same oscillators together with an **odd central** element $\kappa$, $\kappa^2=0$, with the single modified family of relations
$$[b_j^s,a^\sigma]=b_j^s a^\sigma-a^\sigma b_j^s=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\,\kappa,\qquad\sigma,s\in\{+,-\},\ j=1,\dots,n. \tag{1.1}$$
Because $\kappa^2=0$, every element of $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is uniquely $P_0+\kappa P_1$ with $P_0,P_1$ normal-ordered polynomials (PBW), and we always write $\kappa$ **on the left**. Moving $\kappa$ past a homogeneous element $P$ costs the sign $(-1)^{p(P)}$.

### 1.2 The algebra $\mathfrak g=C(n+1)$ inside $W$
Let $Q\subset W$ be the span of $1$ and of all quadratic monomials. $Q$ is closed under the super-commutator and
$$\mathfrak g:=\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{sym}(x,y)\},\qquad \operatorname{sym}(x,y)=\tfrac12\big(xy+(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}yx\big),$$
is a subalgebra, $Q=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbf C\,1$, and $\mathfrak g\cong\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$. Concretely, the basis used in all artifacts is

| name | element of $W$ | parity | weight $(\varepsilon;\delta_1,\dots,\delta_n)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $N_a$ | $a^+a^--\tfrac12=\operatorname{sym}(a^+,a^-)$ | 0 | $0$ |
| $N_k$ | $b_k^+b_k^-+\tfrac12=\operatorname{sym}(b_k^+,b_k^-)$ | 0 | $0$ |
| $E_{\pm2\delta_k}$ | $(b_k^\pm)^2$ | 0 | $\pm2\delta_k$ |
| $E_{\pm\delta_k\pm\delta_l}$ ($k<l$), $E_{\delta_k-\delta_l}$ ($k\ne l$) | $b_k^\pm b_l^\pm$, $b_k^+b_l^-$ | 0 | as named |
| $E_{\pm\varepsilon\pm\delta_k}$ | $a^\pm b_k^\pm$ (fermion on the left) | 1 | as named |

Dimension $(2n^2+n+1)\,|\,4n$: $8=4|4$, $19=11|8$, $34=22|12$ for $n=1,2,3$, as in `Cn1_definition.md`. The simple generators of that document lie in $\mathfrak g$ (e.g. $H_{n+1}=-b_n^+b_n^--\tfrac12=-N_n$, $H_1=N_a+N_1$), so this is the same algebra. $\mathfrak g$ is the **unique** subalgebra complement of $\mathbf C1$ in $Q$: $\mathfrak g$ is simple, hence $\mathfrak g=[Q,Q]$. The projection $\pi:Q\to\mathfrak g$ along $\mathbf C1$ is therefore canonical; it is "passing to the adjoint representation", and $\pi\circ\operatorname{ad}X=\operatorname{ad}X\circ\pi$ for $X\in\mathfrak g$.

The same expressions define elements of $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$ (we keep the normal ordering "fermions left").

### 1.3 The cocycle $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ and the scalar part
For $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$ compute the super-commutator in $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$:
$$[X,Y]_{\mathrm{gb}}=[X,Y]_0+\kappa\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y),\qquad \Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)\in Q. \tag{1.2}$$
(Only the mixed relations (1.1) are deformed, so the $\kappa$-free part is the undeformed bracket; the $\kappa$-part is again of degree $\le 2$.) Decompose $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)=\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)+s_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)\cdot 1$ with $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)=\pi\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)\in\mathfrak g$.

* $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}:\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ is the $\mathfrak g$-valued ("adjoint representation") 2-cochain of `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md`; its coefficients $\gamma_{abc}$ are linear in $\mathrm{gb}$.
* $s_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is the **scalar part**: this is what the phrase "up to scalar" in `C_coboundary_definition.md` discards. §3.4 shows exactly what it is.

Parity bookkeeping: $\kappa$ is odd, so $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)$ has parity $p(X)+p(Y)+1$ — $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is an **odd** cochain with values in the adjoint module (equivalently an even cochain with values in $\Pi\mathfrak g$), and it is super-skew: $\gamma(Y,X)=-(-1)^{p(X)p(Y)}\gamma(X,Y)$.

### 1.4 Where the coboundary formula comes from (and its sign)
Let $L=\mathfrak g\oplus\kappa\mathfrak g$ with $[X,\kappa Y]=(-1)^{p(X)}\kappa[X,Y]$, $[\kappa X,Y]=\kappa[X,Y]$, $[\kappa X,\kappa Y]=0$ (these are forced by $\kappa$ odd, central, $\kappa^2=0$). For an odd linear $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ put $\psi_f(X)=X+\kappa f(X)$. Using $X\kappa=(-1)^{p(X)}\kappa X$ and $p(f(Y))=p(Y)+1$ one finds
$$[\psi_fX,\psi_fY]-\psi_f([X,Y]_0)=\kappa\Big((-1)^{p(X)}[X,f(Y)]-(-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)}[Y,f(X)]-f([X,Y])\Big)=\kappa\,(\delta f)(X,Y),$$
which is precisely the formula of `C_coboundary_definition.md`. Hence $\gamma=\delta f$ means: the deformed bracket $[\,,]_0+\kappa\gamma$ is the transport of the undeformed bracket by the change of basis $X\mapsto X+\kappa f(X)$ of $L$; this is the meaning of "trivial". Both $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ and $\delta f$ are odd 2-cocycles for the same complex (§4), so the two deformation structures of the issue are directly comparable.

## 2. Strategy

The deformation (1.1) is a deformation of the *oscillator* algebra, not of $\mathfrak g$ directly. The whole proof rests on one observation:

> **The odd central $\kappa$ allows one to shift the fermionic oscillators by $\kappa$ times bosonic oscillators, and such a shift absorbs the mixed relations (1.1) completely.**

Pushing this shift down to the quadratic elements gives the map $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$. Independently, a grading argument (§4) shows that $C(n+1)$ has no odd adjoint cohomology at all.

## 3. Constructive proof

### 3.1 The absorbing shift
For $\sigma\in\{+,-\}$ define the even elements
$$d_\sigma:=\sum_{j=1}^n\Big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}\,b_j^+-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}\,b_j^-\Big)\in W, \tag{3.1}$$
and the "primed" oscillators in $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$:
$$a'^{\sigma}:=a^\sigma+\kappa\,d_\sigma,\qquad b'^{\,s}_k:=b_k^s .$$

**Lemma 3.1.** The primed oscillators satisfy the *undeformed* relations. Hence $\varphi:a^\sigma\mapsto a'^\sigma,\ b_k^s\mapsto b_k^s$ extends to an algebra homomorphism $\varphi:W\to W_{\mathrm{gb}}$.

*Proof.* Using (1.1) and $[d_\sigma,\cdot]$ with the CCR,
$$[a'^\sigma,b_j^s]=[a^\sigma,b_j^s]_{\mathrm{gb}}+\kappa[d_\sigma,b_j^s]
=\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa+\kappa\big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}[b_j^+,b_j^s]-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}[b_j^-,b_j^s]\big).$$
For $s=+$ the bracket term is $-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}[b_j^-,b_j^+]\kappa=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}\kappa$; for $s=-$ it is $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}[b_j^+,b_j^-]\kappa=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}\kappa$. Either way the sum is $0$. Next, $\{a'^\sigma,a'^\tau\}=\{a^\sigma,a^\tau\}+\kappa[d_\sigma,a^\tau]_{\pm}+\dots$, and every extra term is either $\kappa\cdot\kappa=0$ or $\kappa$ times a bracket $[b,a]\propto\kappa$, hence $0$; so $\{a'^-,a'^+\}=1$, $(a'^\pm)^2=0$. Boson–boson relations are untouched. Since $W$ is presented by exactly these relations, $\varphi$ is a homomorphism. $\square$

(The shift is the unique one with $b$'s fixed: the symplectic form on the bosons is non-degenerate, so (3.1) is forced. One could also shift the bosons by $\kappa\cdot$(fermions); this changes $f$ by an inner odd derivation, cf. §4.)

### 3.2 Pushing the shift down to $\mathfrak g$
For $Z\in\mathfrak g\subset W$ write $\varphi(Z)=Z+\kappa F(Z)$ with $F:\mathfrak g\to Q$ linear. Explicitly, since $\varphi(xy)=xy+\kappa\big(d_xy+(-1)^{p(x)}x\,d_y\big)$ with $d_{a^\sigma}=d_\sigma$, $d_b=0$:
$$F\big(\operatorname{sym}(b,b')\big)=0,\qquad F(a^\sigma b_k^t)=d_\sigma b_k^t,\qquad F(N_a)=-a^+d_-+d_+a^- . \tag{3.2}$$
$F$ reverses parity. Write $F=F_{\mathfrak g}+\lambda\cdot 1$ with $F_{\mathfrak g}=\pi F:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ and $\lambda:\mathfrak g\to\mathbf C$ (the scalar part; from (3.2), $\lambda(a^\sigma b_k^t)=-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,k,t}$, all other $\lambda=0$).

**Proposition 3.2.** $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=-\delta F$ as $Q$-valued cochains (with $\delta$ given by the same formula, brackets with $1$ being zero). Consequently
$$\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}},\qquad f_{\mathrm{gb}}:=-\pi F:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\ \text{(odd)},\qquad\text{and}\qquad s_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)=\lambda([X,Y]_0).$$

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 3.1 to $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$: $[\varphi X,\varphi Y]_{\mathrm{gb}}=\varphi([X,Y]_0)$. Expand both sides with $\kappa^2=0$, exactly as in §1.4 (the computation there did not use that $f$ is $\mathfrak g$-valued):
$$\text{LHS}=[X,Y]_0+\kappa\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)+\kappa\Big((-1)^{p(X)}[X,F(Y)]-(-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)}[Y,F(X)]\Big),\qquad
\text{RHS}=[X,Y]_0+\kappa F([X,Y]_0).$$
Comparing $\kappa$-parts gives $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=-\delta F$. Now $[X,F(Y)]=[X,F_{\mathfrak g}(Y)]\in\mathfrak g$ (scalars are central), so the $\mathfrak g$-component of $-\delta F$ is $\delta(-F_{\mathfrak g})$ and the scalar component is $+\lambda([X,Y]_0)$. $\square$

### 3.3 The explicit map $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$
From (3.2) and $f_{\mathrm{gb}}=-\pi F$:
$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{gb}}\big(\operatorname{sym}(b_k^s,b_l^t)\big)&=0\qquad(\text{all of }\mathfrak{sp}(2n)),\\
f_{\mathrm{gb}}\big(E_{\sigma\varepsilon+t\delta_k}\big)=f_{\mathrm{gb}}(a^\sigma b_k^t)&=-\sum_{j=1}^n\Big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}\operatorname{sym}(b_j^+,b_k^t)-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}\operatorname{sym}(b_j^-,b_k^t)\Big),\\
f_{\mathrm{gb}}(N_a)&=\sum_{j=1}^n\Big(\mathrm{gb}_{-,j,-}\,a^+b_j^+-\mathrm{gb}_{-,j,+}\,a^+b_j^--\mathrm{gb}_{+,j,-}\,a^-b_j^++\mathrm{gb}_{+,j,+}\,a^-b_j^-\Big).
\end{aligned}}$$
$f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is odd (it maps even $N_a$ to odd root vectors and odd root vectors to $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$), linear in $\mathrm{gb}$, and $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ holds **exactly** (not only up to a scalar factor). This proves the Theorem for all $n$ and all $\mathrm{gb}$.

For $n=1$, with $g_{\sigma s}:=\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,1,s}$:
$f(N_a)=g_{--}E_{\varepsilon+\delta}-g_{-+}E_{\varepsilon-\delta}-g_{+-}E_{-\varepsilon+\delta}+g_{++}E_{-\varepsilon-\delta}$,
$f(E_{\varepsilon+\delta})=-g_{+-}E_{2\delta}+g_{++}N_1$, $f(E_{\varepsilon-\delta})=-g_{+-}N_1+g_{++}E_{-2\delta}$,
$f(E_{-\varepsilon+\delta})=-g_{--}E_{2\delta}+g_{-+}N_1$, $f(E_{-\varepsilon-\delta})=-g_{--}N_1+g_{-+}E_{-2\delta}$, $f(\mathfrak{sp}(2))=0$.

A sample of $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ for $n=1$ (full list of 84 non-zero entries in `artifacts/cn1_n1.json`; 456 and 1308 for $n=2,3$):
$\gamma(N_a,E_{\varepsilon+\delta})=-g_{++}N_a$, $\gamma(E_{\varepsilon+\delta},E_{\varepsilon+\delta})=2g_{++}E_{\varepsilon+\delta}$, $\gamma(E_{2\delta},E_{\varepsilon-\delta})=-2g_{++}N_1$ (with scalar part $s=+g_{++}$), $\gamma(N_a,N_1)=-g_{--}E_{\varepsilon+\delta}-g_{-+}E_{\varepsilon-\delta}+g_{+-}E_{-\varepsilon+\delta}+g_{++}E_{-\varepsilon-\delta}$.
Hand check of the third entry: $b^+b^+a^+=a^+b^+b^+-2g_{++}\kappa b^+$ by (1.1), so $[E_{2\delta},E_{\varepsilon-\delta}]_{\mathrm{gb}}=-2a^+b^+-2g_{++}\kappa\,b^+b^-=-2E_{\varepsilon+\delta}+\kappa(-2g_{++}N_1+g_{++})$; and $\delta f(E_{2\delta},E_{\varepsilon-\delta})=[E_{2\delta},f(E_{\varepsilon-\delta})]-f([E_{2\delta},E_{\varepsilon-\delta}]_0)=g_{++}[(b^+)^2,(b^-)^2]+2f(E_{\varepsilon+\delta})=g_{++}(-4N_1)+2g_{++}N_1=-2g_{++}N_1$. $\checkmark$

### 3.4 Handling of the "adjoint representation / up to scalar" condition
`C_coboundary_definition.md` asks for $\gamma=\delta f$ "as an adjoint representation … up to scalar". The analysis above makes this precise and shows it is *exactly* the right formulation:

1. **Adjoint representation.** The $\kappa$-coefficient $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)$ naturally lives in $Q=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbf C1\subset W_{\mathrm{gb}}$, not in $\mathfrak g$. "As an adjoint representation" means: compare in $\mathfrak g$, i.e. apply the canonical projection $\pi$ (kill the identity component). $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}:=\pi\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is then an honest 2-cocycle with values in the adjoint module (parity-shifted), and $\pi$ is canonical because $\mathfrak g=[Q,Q]$.
2. **Up to scalar (as "modulo scalars").** With $f$ required to be $\mathfrak g$-valued, $\delta f$ has no scalar component, whereas $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ does ($s_{\mathrm{gb}}\neq0$ for $\mathrm{gb}\neq0$, e.g. $s(E_{2\delta},E_{\varepsilon-\delta})=g_{++}$). So an equality $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f$ *in $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$* with $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ is impossible, and the clause "up to scalar" is necessary. Proposition 3.2 shows the discarded part is harmless: $s_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)=\lambda([X,Y]_0)$ is itself the coboundary of the scalar-valued 1-cochain $-\lambda$, so if one allows $f$ to take values in $Q=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbf C1$ (i.e. $f=-F$) the equality $\Gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta(-F)$ holds on the nose, scalars included. Either way the deformation is trivial.
3. **Up to scalar (as "up to a scalar factor").** If the clause is read as $\gamma=c\,\delta f$ for some $c\in\mathbf C^\times$, nothing changes: $\delta$ is linear, so $\gamma=c\,\delta f\iff\gamma=\delta(cf)$; our $f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ realises $c=1$.
4. **Independence of choices.** Replacing the embedding $Z\mapsto Z+\mu(Z)1$ of $\mathfrak g$ into $Q$ (e.g. using $b^+b^-$ instead of $\operatorname{sym}$, or $b\,a$ instead of $a\,b$ in the odd generators, which differ by $\kappa\cdot$scalar) changes $\Gamma$ only by scalar-valued terms. Thus $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$, the statement $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$, and the conclusion do not depend on such conventions; only $s_{\mathrm{gb}}$ does — again exactly what "up to scalar" is meant to absorb.
5. **Sign of $\delta$.** With the sign convention of the document, the correct map is $f_{\mathrm{gb}}=-\pi F$ (not $+\pi F$); the computations in §6 were essential to pin this sign down.

## 4. Cohomological proof: $H^\bullet(\mathfrak g;\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$

Let $h_0:=N_a=\operatorname{sym}(a^+,a^-)$, the generator of the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ factor of $\mathfrak g_{\bar0}=\mathfrak{so}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$. Then $\operatorname{ad}h_0$ is diagonalisable on $\mathfrak g$ with eigenvalues $-1,0,1$ (the $\varepsilon$-coordinate of the weight):
$$\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_1,\qquad \mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak g_{\bar0},\quad \mathfrak g_{\pm1}\subset\mathfrak g_{\bar1},\ \mathfrak g_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak g_1=\mathfrak g_{\bar1}.$$
In particular
$$\text{(†)}\qquad \text{the }\operatorname{ad}h_0\text{-eigenvalue of a homogeneous element}\equiv\text{its parity}\pmod 2 .$$
(Verified for $n=1,2,3$: `parity_equals_epsilon_weight_mod_2 = true`.)

**Lemma 4.1 (weight concentration).** Let $M$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g$-module on which $h_0$ acts semisimply. Then the inclusion of the $\operatorname{ad}h_0$-weight-zero subcomplex $C^\bullet(\mathfrak g;M)_0\hookrightarrow C^\bullet(\mathfrak g;M)$ is a quasi-isomorphism.

*Proof.* $h_0$ is even, so the Cartan homotopy formula $L_{h_0}=d\iota_{h_0}+\iota_{h_0}d$ holds on the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of the Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak g$ with coefficients in $M$ (no signs appear for even elements), and $d$ commutes with $L_{h_0}$. The complex splits into $L_{h_0}$-eigenspaces $C^\bullet_\mu$, each a subcomplex. If $\mu\neq0$ and $\omega\in C^p_\mu$ is a cocycle, then $\omega=\mu^{-1}L_{h_0}\omega=d(\mu^{-1}\iota_{h_0}\omega)$ is a coboundary. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2.** $H^p(\mathfrak g;\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$ for all $p\ge0$ and all $n\ge1$. Equivalently, the odd part of $H^p(\mathfrak g;\mathfrak g)$ vanishes.

*Proof.* Take $M=\mathfrak g$ (adjoint). By Lemma 4.1 it suffices to show that the weight-zero subcomplex contains no odd cochains. Let $\omega\in C^p_0$ be homogeneous of parity $p(\omega)$ and let $X_1,\dots,X_p$ be homogeneous with $\operatorname{ad}h_0$-weights $\mu_i$. Then $\omega(X_1,\dots,X_p)$ has weight $\sum\mu_i$ (weight zero means $\omega$ is $\operatorname{ad}h_0$-equivariant) and parity $p(\omega)+\sum p(X_i)$. By (†) applied to the $X_i$ and to the value, $\sum\mu_i\equiv p(\omega)+\sum p(X_i)\equiv p(\omega)+\sum\mu_i\pmod 2$, hence $p(\omega)=0$. So $(C^\bullet_0)_{\bar1}=0$ and the odd cohomology vanishes. $\square$

**Corollaries.**
* ($p=2$) Every odd 2-cocycle is a coboundary. Since $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is an odd 2-cocycle (the deformed bracket satisfies the super-Jacobi identity in $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$, verified symbolically), $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f$ for some odd $f$ — a second, non-constructive proof of the Theorem for every $n$. Also: there is **no** non-trivial odd adjoint deformation of $C(n+1)$ from any source, not only from the $\mathrm{gb}$ family.
* ($p=1$) $H^1(\mathfrak g;\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$: every odd derivation of $\mathfrak g$ is inner. Hence $f$ with $\delta f=\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is unique up to $\operatorname{ad}Y$, $Y\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ (a $4n$-dimensional ambiguity; this is the $\ker\delta^1$ of dimension $4n$ found numerically, and it explains why the independent linear solver in §6 returns a different, equally valid, $f$ than §3.3).
* The argument uses only the $\mathbf Z$-grading $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_1$ with $\mathfrak g_{\bar1}=\mathfrak g_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak g_1$ induced by an inner even element. This is the type-I structure of $C(n+1)$; it is precisely the feature that distinguishes $C(n+1)$ from $B(0,n)=\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$, where no such inner grading exists.

## 5. Non-degeneracy: $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}\ne0$ for $\mathrm{gb}\neq0$

$\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is linear in $\mathrm{gb}$. The entry $\gamma(N_a,E_{\sigma\varepsilon+s\delta_j})=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}N_a$ (direct computation: $[N_a,a^\sigma b_j^s]_{\mathrm{gb}}$ picks up $-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa\,a^+a^-$ from (1.1)) shows that the $4n$ cocycles $\gamma_{e_{\sigma,j,s}}$ are linearly independent; computationally $\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{gb}\mapsto\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}})=4n$ for $n=1,2,3$. So **no non-zero $\mathrm{gb}$ gives a vanishing cocycle, and no non-zero $\mathrm{gb}$ gives a non-trivial one**: the answer to "which non-zero configurations satisfy $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f$" is *all of them*.

## 6. Computational verification ($n=1,2,3$)

### 6.1 What `scripts/cn1_deformation.py` does
1. Implements $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$ from scratch: normal-ordering rewriting with the CAR/CCR, the deformed rule $b_j^sa^\sigma=a^\sigma b_j^s-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa$, $\kappa$ odd central (sign $(-1)^{\#\text{odd}}$ when moved left), $\kappa^2=0$. The $4n$ parameters $\mathrm{gb}$ are **sympy symbols**; all results are exact and symbolic.
2. Builds the basis of §1.2, decomposes every bracket $[Z_i,Z_j]_{\mathrm{gb}}$ into $c_{ij}^k$, $s^0_{ij}$ ($=0$), $\gamma_{ij}^k(\mathrm{gb})$, $s^1_{ij}(\mathrm{gb})$.
3. Checks: closure of $\mathfrak g$ without scalars, super skew-symmetry, super-Jacobi, trivial centre, weights, (†), $\gamma$ linear in $\mathrm{gb}$, $\gamma$ odd, $\gamma$ a 2-cocycle (first-order super-Jacobi on $L$).
4. Builds $a'^\sigma$ and checks Lemma 3.1 on all oscillator pairs; builds $\varphi(Z_i)$, checks $[\varphi Z_i,\varphi Z_j]_{\mathrm{gb}}=\sum_kc_{ij}^k\varphi(Z_k)$ exactly in $W_{\mathrm{gb}}$; extracts $F$, $f_{\mathrm{gb}}=-\pi F$, $\lambda$; checks $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ symbolically and $s^1_{\mathrm{gb}}(X,Y)=\lambda([X,Y]_0)$.
5. **Independently** sets up the linear system $\delta\phi=\gamma_{e}$ for the unknown odd matrix $\phi_{mj}$ (without using §3.3), for each coordinate direction $e$ of $\mathrm{gb}$, solves it with exact rational elimination, and re-substitutes the solution.
6. Computes $\operatorname{rank}(\mathrm{gb}\mapsto\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}})$.
7. Computes $\dim H^1$ and $\dim H^2$ of the odd adjoint complex: on the weight-zero subcomplex (Theorem 4.2) and, for $n=1,2$, on the **full** complex ($d^2$ = first-order Jacobi map, $B^2=\operatorname{im}\delta$), as a check of Lemma 4.1.

`scripts/verify_artifacts.py` re-derives (A) the Lie superalgebra axioms, (B) the cocycle property, (C) $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$, (D) the scalar identity, (E) injectivity, **using only the JSON files** and `fractions.Fraction` — no sympy, no oscillator algebra.

## 7. Conclusion

* **Triviality condition on $\mathrm{gb}$: none.** For every $n\ge1$ and every $\mathrm{gb}\in\mathbf C^{4n}$, $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}=\delta f_{\mathrm{gb}}$ with the explicit odd map of §3.3; the inhomogeneous deformation of $C(n+1)$ is always trivial (equivalent, via $X\mapsto X+\kappa f_{\mathrm{gb}}(X)$, to the undeformed abelian extension $\mathfrak g\oplus\kappa\mathfrak g$).
* The "adjoint representation / up to scalar" clause is exactly the statement that the comparison is made after the canonical projection $Q=\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbf C1\to\mathfrak g$; the discarded scalar part is $\lambda([X,Y]_0)$ and is itself exact, so nothing is lost.
* $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}\neq0$ for $\mathrm{gb}\neq0$: the deformation is non-zero but cohomologically trivial.
* Structurally, the reason is $H^\bullet(\mathfrak g;\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$, forced by the inner $\mathbf Z$-grading $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak g_0\oplus\mathfrak g_1$ of the type-I algebra $C(n+1)$ (eigenvalues of $\operatorname{ad}N_a$) whose parity agrees with the super-parity.
* Everything is verified for $n=1,2,3$ with exact arithmetic and symbolic parameters, and the artifacts can be re-checked without the generating code.
