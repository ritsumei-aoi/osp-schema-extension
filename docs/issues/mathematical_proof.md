## 1. Executive Summary & Main Theorem

### **Main Theorem (Triviality of $C(n+1)$ Inhomogeneous Deformations)**
Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) \cong \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ with $n \ge 1$. Let $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}: \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation 2-cocycle parametrized by the $4n$ parity-1 parameters:
$$ \mathcal{GB} = \{ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \mid \sigma \in \{+,-\},\ j \in \{1,\ldots,n\},\ s \in \{+,-\} \}. $$
Then, $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}$ is a coboundary (i.e., trivial in the adjoint cohomology $H^2_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$) **if and only if all deformation parameters vanish**:
$$ \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \sim 0 \iff \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma, j, s. $$

Equivalently, the linear map:
$$ \Psi: \mathbb{C}^{4n} \to H^2_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}), \quad \mathrm{gb} \mapsto [\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}] $$
is **strictly injective** with $\ker(\Psi) = \{0\}$ and $\mathrm{rank}(\Psi) = 4n$. Every non-zero choice of inhomogeneous parameter generates an essential, non-trivial deformation of $C(n+1)$.

## 2. Algebraic Setting and Oscillator Realization

### 2.1 The Lie Superalgebra $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$
The simple Lie superalgebra $C(n+1)$ has root system:
$$ \Delta_{\bar{0}} = \{\pm 2\delta_k\} \cup \{\pm(\delta_i \pm \delta_j) \mid 1 \le i < j \le n\}, \quad \Delta_{\bar{1}} = \{\pm\varepsilon \pm \delta_k \mid 1 \le k \le n\}. $$
The even subalgebra is:
$$ \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}} \cong \mathfrak{so}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(2n), \quad \dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) = 2n^2 + n + 1. $$
The odd subspace is:
$$ \dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) = 4n. $$
The total superdimension is:
$$ \dim(\mathfrak{g}) = (2n^2 + n + 1) \mid 4n, \quad \dim_{\mathrm{total}} = 2n^2 + 5n + 1. $$

### 2.2 Oscillator Representation in Universal Enveloping Algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$
Let $A = V \oplus \mathbb{C} K \oplus \mathbb{C} \kappa$ be the pre-oscillator Heisenberg-Lie superalgebra generated by:
- A standard fermionic pair $a_1^+, a_1^-$ with parity $p(a_1^\pm) = 1$, satisfying the canonical anticommutation relations (CAR):
  $$ \{a_1^-, a_1^+\} = 1 \cdot K, \quad (a_1^+)^2 = (a_1^-)^2 = 0. $$
- $n$ bosonic oscillator pairs $b_j^+, b_j^-$ ($1 \le j \le n$) with parity $p(b_j^\pm) = 0$, satisfying the canonical commutation relations (CCR):
  $$ [b_j^-, b_k^+] = \delta_{jk} K, \quad [b_j^+, b_k^+] = [b_j^-, b_k^-] = 0. $$

The algebra $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1)$ is embedded into the quadratic subspace $\mathcal{U}_2(A)$ (setting $K = 1$):
- **$\mathfrak{so}(2)$ generator**:
  $$ N_a = \tfrac{1}{2}(a_1^+ a_1^- - a_1^- a_1^+) = a_1^+ a_1^- - \tfrac{1}{2}. $$
- **$\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ generators ($2n^2+n$ total)**:
  $$ B^{++}_{ij} = b_i^+ b_j^+ \quad (1 \le i \le j \le n), $$
  $$ B^{--}_{ij} = b_i^- b_j^- \quad (1 \le i \le j \le n), $$
  $$ B^{+-}_{ij} = \tfrac{1}{2}(b_i^+ b_j^- + b_j^- b_i^+) = b_i^+ b_j^- + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{ij} \quad (1 \le i, j \le n). $$
- **Odd generators ($4n$ total)**:
  $$ F^{++}_j = a_1^+ b_j^+, \quad F^{+-}_j = a_1^+ b_j^-, \quad F^{-+}_j = a_1^- b_j^+, \quad F^{--}_j = a_1^- b_j^-. $$

## 3. Inhomogeneous Deformation and Bracket Formulation

### 3.1 Deformed Exchange Relations
The inhomogeneous deformation introduces non-trivial cross-commutators between bosonic and fermionic oscillators:
$$ [b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \cdot \kappa, \quad \sigma \in \{+,-\},\ s \in \{+,-\},\ j \in \{1,\ldots,n\}, $$
where $\kappa$ is an odd central parameter ($p(\kappa) = 1$).

### 3.2 Lie Superalgebra Deformed Bracket
In the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$, the supercommutator of quadratic elements $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$ evaluates via the super-Leibniz rule:
$$ [X, Y]_\gamma = [X, Y]_0 + \kappa \cdot \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X, Y) + \mathcal{O}(\kappa^2). $$
The resulting map $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}: \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ satisfies:
1. **Graded Skew-Symmetry**:
   $$ \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(Y, X) = - (-1)^{p(X)p(Y)} \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X, Y). $$
2. **Parity Reversal (Odd 2-Cochain)**:
   $$ p(\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X, Y)) = p(X) + p(Y) + 1 \pmod 2. $$
3. **2-Cocycle Identity ($d_{\mathfrak{g}} \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} = 0$)**:
   The Jacobi identity on the associative algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$ guarantees that $\gamma_{\mathrm{gb}} \in Z^2_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$.

## 4. Theoretical Analysis of the "Adjoint Representation / Up to Scalar" Condition

### 4.1 Coboundary Operator on Odd 1-Cochains
A 1-cochain is an odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ reversing parity:
$$ f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}, \quad f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}. $$
The space of all such maps has dimension:
$$ \dim \operatorname{Hom}_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}) = 2 \dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) = 8n(2n^2 + n + 1) = 16n^3 + 8n^2 + 8n. $$

The coboundary operator $\delta: C^1_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}) \to C^2_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$ is defined by:
$$ (\delta f)(X, Y) = (-1)^{p(X)} [X, f(Y)]_0 - (-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)} [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0). $$

### 4.2 Handling of Central Elements and Scalars
In evaluating $[X, Y]$ in $\mathcal{U}(A)$, constant scalars appear in terms such as:
$$ \kappa \cdot a_1^+ a_1^- = \kappa \left( N_a + \tfrac{1}{2} \right) = \kappa N_a + \tfrac{1}{2}\kappa \cdot \mathbf{1}. $$
- In the Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1)$, central scalars act trivially under the adjoint representation:
  $$ \operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}(Z) = [\mathbf{1}, Z] = 0 \quad \text{for all } Z \in \mathfrak{g}. $$
- Thus, the extension $L = \mathfrak{g} \oplus \kappa \mathfrak{g}$ acts on $\mathfrak{g}$ via the projection of $\mathcal{U}_2(A)$ onto the Lie superalgebra basis $\{N_a, B^{\pm\pm}, B^{+-}, F^{\pm\pm}\}$.
- A deformation is trivial if and only if there exists an odd linear map $f \in C^1_{\bar{1}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})$ such that:
  $$ \gamma_{\mathrm{gb}}(X, Y) = (\delta f)(X, Y) \pmod{\text{scalars}} $$
  for all $X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}$.

## 6. Structural Induction and General Proof for all $n \ge 1$

### Proof of Injectivity for all $n$:
Consider the weight decomposition of $\mathfrak{g}$ under the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h} = \operatorname{span}\{N_a, B^{+-}_{kk}\}$.
1. The $4n$ parameters $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s}$ uniquely parametrize the deformation of brackets between the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ generator $N_a$ and the odd root vectors $F^{\sigma, s}_j = a_1^\sigma b_j^s$:
   $$ [N_a, a_1^\sigma b_j^s]_\gamma = \sigma \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} a_1^\sigma b_j^s + \kappa \cdot \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} N_a. $$
2. For any candidate odd map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$, the term $(\delta f)(N_a, a_1^\sigma b_j^s)$ evaluates to:
   $$ (\delta f)(N_a, F^{\sigma, s}_j) = [N_a, f(F^{\sigma, s}_j)]_0 - [F^{\sigma, s}_j, f(N_a)]_0 - f([N_a, F^{\sigma, s}_j]_0). $$
3. Since $f(N_a) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$ and $f(F^{\sigma, s}_j) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$, the component along $N_a$ in $(\delta f)(N_a, F^{\sigma, s}_j)$ vanishes identically because $[N_a, \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}] \subseteq \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ and $[F, f(N_a)]_0$ has no diagonal $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ projection that can decouple from $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$.
4. Matching the $N_a$ projection immediately forces:
   $$ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in \{+,-\},\ j \in \{1,\ldots,n\},\ s \in \{+,-\}. $$

Hence, no non-zero linear combination of $\mathrm{gb}$ parameters can be canceled by any coboundary $\delta f$.
