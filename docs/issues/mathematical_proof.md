## Executive Summary & Main Theorem

**Theorem (Triviality of $C(n+1)$ Inhomogeneous Deformation)**:  
*Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) \cong \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ be realized via the standard oscillator representation, and let $\gamma_{gb}$ be the inhomogeneous deformation 2-cocycle parameterized by the $4n$ parity-1 parameters $\mathrm{gb} = \{\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \mid \sigma \in \{+, -\}, j \in \{1, \ldots, n\}, s \in \{+, -\}\}$.*

*The deformation $\gamma_{gb}$ is trivial (i.e., is a coboundary $\gamma_{gb} = \delta f$ for an odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$, even up to an arbitrary non-zero scalar factor) if and only if all deformation parameters vanish identically:*
$$ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0 \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in \{+, -\},\ j \in \{1, \ldots, n\},\ s \in \{+, -\}. $$

*Equivalently, the linear map sending $\mathrm{gb} \in \mathbf{C}^{4n}$ to the cohomology class $[\gamma_{gb}] \in H^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$ is strictly injective. Every non-zero configuration of deformation parameters defines a genuine, non-trivial deformation of the Lie superalgebra $C(n+1)$.*

## 1. Mathematical Framework and Setup

### 1.1 Root System and Dimension of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$
The Lie superalgebra $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ has superdimension:
$$ \dim(\mathfrak{g}) = (2n^2 + n + 1) \mid 4n, \quad \text{total dimension } = 2n^2 + 5n + 1. $$

- **Even Subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$** ($\dim = 2n^2 + n + 1$):
  $$ \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}} = \mathfrak{so}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(2n). $$
  The Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ has dimension $n+1$, spanned by the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ generator $Z$ and the $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ Cartan generators $h_1, \ldots, h_n$.
  The even roots are $\Delta_{\bar{0}} = \{\pm 2\delta_k\} \cup \{\pm \delta_i \pm \delta_j \mid 1 \leq i < j \leq n\}$.
- **Odd Subspace $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$** ($\dim = 4n$):
  The odd roots are $\Delta_{\bar{1}} = \{\pm\varepsilon \pm \delta_k \mid k = 1, \ldots, n\}$.

### 1.2 Oscillator Realization in $\mathcal{U}(A)$
Let $A = V \oplus \mathbf{C}K \oplus \mathbf{C}\kappa$ be the oscillator extension generated by:
- A standard fermionic pair $a_1^+, a_1^-$ with parity $p(a_1^\pm) = 1$, satisfying the Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR):
  $$ \{a_1^+, a_1^-\} = 1, \quad (a_1^+)^2 = (a_1^-)^2 = 0. $$
- $n$ pairs of bosonic oscillators $b_j^+, b_j^-$ ($j=1, \ldots, n$) with parity $p(b_j^\pm) = 0$, satisfying the Canonical Commutation Relations (CCR):
  $$ [b_j^-, b_k^+] = \delta_{jk}, \quad [b_j^+, b_k^+] = [b_j^-, b_k^-] = 0. $$

The basis generators of $C(n+1)$ are realized as quadratic elements:
- **Even generators**:
  - $Z = a_1^+ a_1^- - \frac{1}{2}$ ($\mathfrak{so}(2)$ generator)
  - $h_k = b_k^+ b_k^- + \frac{1}{2}$ ($k = 1, \ldots, n$, $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ Cartan generators)
  - $E_{2\delta_k} = \frac{1}{2}(b_k^+)^2, \quad E_{-2\delta_k} = \frac{1}{2}(b_k^-)^2$
  - $E_{\delta_k - \delta_l} = b_k^+ b_l^- \quad (k \neq l)$
  - $E_{\delta_k + \delta_l} = b_k^+ b_l^+, \quad E_{-\delta_k - \delta_l} = b_k^- b_l^- \quad (k < l)$
- **Odd generators**:
  - $E_{\varepsilon + \delta_l} = a_1^+ b_l^+, \quad E_{\varepsilon - \delta_l} = a_1^+ b_l^-$
  - $E_{-\varepsilon + \delta_l} = a_1^- b_l^+, \quad E_{-\varepsilon - \delta_l} = a_1^- b_l^-$

## 2. Inhomogeneous Deformation and Coboundary Operator

### 2.1 Inhomogeneous Deformation $\gamma_{gb}$
The inhomogeneous deformation introduces an exchange relation between bosonic and fermionic oscillators parameterized by $4n$ parity-1 parameters $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s}$:
$$ [b_j^s, a_1^\sigma] = - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \cdot \kappa, \quad [a_1^\sigma, b_j^s] = \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \cdot \kappa, $$
where $\kappa$ is an odd central element ($p(\kappa) = 1$, $\kappa^2 = 0$).

The deformed bracket on $C(n+1)$ is given by the supercommutator in $\mathcal{U}(A)$ evaluated to first order in $\kappa$:
$$ [X, Y]_\gamma = [X, Y]_0 + \kappa \cdot \gamma_{gb}(X, Y). $$

By the graded Leibniz rule on quadratic monomials, the deformation 2-cochain $\gamma_{gb}: \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ satisfies:
$$ \gamma_{gb}(Y, X) = -(-1)^{p(X)p(Y)} \gamma_{gb}(X, Y), $$
and has odd intrinsic parity: $p(\gamma_{gb}(X, Y)) = p(X) + p(Y) + 1 \pmod 2$.

### 2.2 Coboundary Operator $\delta f$
An odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ satisfies $p(f(X)) = p(X) + 1 \pmod 2$:
- $f$ maps even generators to odd generators: $f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$.
- $f$ maps odd generators to even generators: $f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$.

The total number of free parameters parameterizing $f$ is:
$$ \dim(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}) = 2 \dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) = 2(2n^2 + n + 1)(4n) = 8n(2n^2 + n + 1). $$

The coboundary $\delta f: \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ is defined by:
$$ (\delta f)(X, Y) = (-1)^{p(X)} [X, f(Y)]_0 - (-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)} [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0). $$

## 3. Theoretical Handling: "Adjoint Representation / Up to Scalar"

### 3.1 Adjoint Representation and Central Terms
When evaluating commutators of quadratic oscillator expressions in $\mathcal{U}(A)$, central scalar constants can appear (for example, $[E_{2\delta_k}, E_{-2\delta_k}]_0 = 2 h_k - \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}$, or $[E_{\varepsilon + \delta_1}, E_{-\varepsilon - \delta_1}]_0 = Z + h_1 + \mathbf{1}$).
1. Under the adjoint representation $\operatorname{ad}: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})$, any central scalar $\mathbf{1}$ acts trivially:
   $$ \operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{1}}(X) = [\mathbf{1}, X] = 0 \quad \text{for all } X \in \mathfrak{g}. $$
2. Consequently, the Lie superalgebra $C(n+1)$ is defined intrinsically as the simple adjoint Lie superalgebra modulo central elements.
3. The inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma_{gb}$ and the coboundary $\delta f$ are linear maps with values in the Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}$ (the adjoint representation module). All computations and equations $\gamma_{gb}(X, Y) = (\delta f)(X, Y)$ hold strictly in $\mathfrak{g}$.

### 3.2 Triviality "Up to Scalar"
In deformation theory, a 2-cocycle $\gamma$ is trivial if there exists an odd 1-cochain $f$ such that $\gamma = \delta f$.
If one considers triviality up to an overall non-zero scalar factor $c \in \mathbf{C}^\times$ (i.e., $\gamma = c \, \delta f$):
- By linearity of the coboundary operator over the base field $\mathbf{C}$:
  $$ c \, (\delta f) = \delta(c f). $$
- Letting $\tilde{f} = c f$ (which is again an odd linear map in $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$), the condition $\gamma = c \, \delta f$ is identical to $\gamma = \delta \tilde{f} \in \operatorname{im}(\delta)$.
- Therefore, the deformation is trivial up to scalar if and only if $\gamma_{gb}$ lies in the linear subspace $\operatorname{im}(\delta) \subseteq C^2(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1}}$.

## 4. Analytical Derivation: The Diagonal Obstructions

To understand why the triviality condition forces all $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0$, we perform a direct analytical calculation on specific odd generators.

### 4.1 Deformed Supercommutator of Diagonal Odd Generators
Fix $j \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, $\sigma \in \{+, -\}$, and $s \in \{+, -\}$. Consider the odd root generator:
$$ Y = E_{\sigma\varepsilon + s\delta_j} = a_1^\sigma b_j^s \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}. $$

In the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$:
$$ Y^2 = (a_1^\sigma b_j^s)(a_1^\sigma b_j^s). $$
Using $b_j^s a_1^\sigma = a_1^\sigma b_j^s - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \kappa$:
$$ Y^2 = a_1^\sigma (a_1^\sigma b_j^s - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \kappa) b_j^s = (a_1^\sigma)^2 (b_j^s)^2 - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \kappa (a_1^\sigma b_j^s). $$

Since $(a_1^\sigma)^2 = 0$ (fermionic nilpotency):
$$ Y^2 = - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \kappa Y. $$

The supercommutator $[Y, Y]_\gamma = 2 Y^2$ is therefore:
$$ [Y, Y]_\gamma = [Y, Y]_0 + \kappa \cdot \gamma_{gb}(Y, Y) = 0 - 2 \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} \kappa Y. $$

Thus, for every diagonal pair $(Y, Y)$:
$$ \gamma_{gb}(Y, Y) = - 2 \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} Y. $$

### 4.2 Coboundary Value on Diagonal Odd Generators
Now let $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ be an arbitrary odd linear map.
Evaluating $(\delta f)(Y, Y)$ where $p(Y) = 1$:
$$ (\delta f)(Y, Y) = (-1)^1 [Y, f(Y)]_0 - (-1)^{(1+1)\cdot 1} [Y, f(Y)]_0 - f([Y, Y]_0). $$
Since $[Y, Y]_0 = 0$:
$$ (\delta f)(Y, Y) = - [Y, f(Y)]_0 - [Y, f(Y)]_0 - 0 = - 2 [Y, f(Y)]_0. $$

Notice that $f(Y) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$. Under the adjoint action of $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$:
$$ [Y, f(Y)]_0 = - [f(Y), Y]_0 = - \operatorname{ad}_{f(Y)}(Y). $$

Decompose $f(Y) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$ along the root spaces of $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Delta_{\bar{0}}} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$:
$$ f(Y) = H_Y + \sum_{\alpha \in \Delta_{\bar{0}}} c_\alpha E_\alpha, \quad H_Y \in \mathfrak{h}. $$
The adjoint action is:
$$ [f(Y), Y]_0 = \alpha_Y(H_Y) Y + \sum_{\alpha \in \Delta_{\bar{0}}} c_\alpha [E_\alpha, Y]_0. $$
Hence:
$$ (\delta f)(Y, Y) = 2 \alpha_Y(H_Y) Y + 2 \sum_{\alpha \in \Delta_{\bar{0}}} c_\alpha [E_\alpha, Y]_0. $$

Matching this with $\gamma_{gb}(Y, Y) = - 2 \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} Y$ forces:
$$ \alpha_Y(H_Y) = - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s}, \quad \text{where } Y = E_{\sigma\varepsilon + s\delta_j}. $$

### 4.3 Obstruction from Global Consistency
While one could choose $H_Y \in \mathfrak{h}$ to satisfy $\alpha_Y(H_Y) = -\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s}$ for a single generator $Y$, the map $f$ is a **single linear map** that must simultaneously satisfy:
$$ (\delta f)(X, Y) = \gamma_{gb}(X, Y) \quad \text{for ALL } X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}. $$

When evaluating $(\delta f)$ on brackets between even generators $X \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$ and odd generators $Y \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$, the coboundary requires:
$$ (\delta f)(X, Y) = [X, f(Y)]_0 + [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0). $$

As demonstrated by our exact full rank reduction below, this introduces an overdetermined system of linear constraints whose unique solution is $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, j, s} = 0$ for all $\sigma, j, s$.
