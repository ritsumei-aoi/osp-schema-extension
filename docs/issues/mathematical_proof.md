# Triviality of the Inhomogeneous Deformation of $C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$

**Status**: resolved (definitive conclusion)
**Scope**: the deformation $\gamma_{gb}$ of `docs/math/C_inhomogeneous_definition.md`, the coboundary
$\delta f$ of `docs/math/C_coboundary_definition.md`, the algebra of `docs/math/Cn1_definition.md`.
**Artifacts**: `artifacts/Cn1_triviality_n{1,2,3}.json`, `artifacts/summary.json`,
`scripts/osp_deformation.py`, `scripts/verify_triviality.py`, `scripts/check_artifacts.py`.

---

## 0. Main result

> **Theorem.** For every $n\ge 1$ and for **every** choice of the $4n$ parameters
> $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\in\mathbb C$ ($\sigma,s\in\{+,-\}$, $1\le j\le n$) the inhomogeneous
> deformation $\gamma_{gb}$ of $\mathfrak g=C(n+1)$ is trivial: there is an odd linear map
> $f:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ with $\gamma_{gb}=\delta f$ in the sense of
> `C_coboundary_definition.md` (equality in the adjoint representation, i.e. modulo
> $\kappa\cdot\mathbb C$).
>
> Consequently the **necessary and sufficient condition for triviality is vacuous**: no non-zero
> configuration of the $\mathrm{gb}$ parameters produces a non-trivial deformation, and the
> set of trivial configurations is all of $\mathbb C^{4n}$.
>
> More strongly, $H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$ (odd second cohomology with adjoint
> coefficients), so *any* inhomogeneous (odd, $\kappa$-linear) deformation of $C(n+1)$ is trivial,
> not only the one produced by the oscillator relations.

Two independent proofs are given. Proof A (Section 3) is constructive: the deformation of the
oscillator algebra is removed by the explicit change of variables
$a_1^\sigma\mapsto a_1^\sigma+\kappa u^\sigma$, which induces the required $f$. Proof B
(Section 4) is cohomological: the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ generator $N$ grades $\mathfrak g$ so that
parity equals degree mod 2, hence no odd cochain has weight zero, hence $H^\bullet(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$
by a Cartan-homotopy formula that is itself written out explicitly and yields a second primitive $f_N$.
Both proofs, the scalar bookkeeping of Section 5, and the vanishing of $H^2$ are verified by exact
rational arithmetic for $n=1,2,3$ (Section 6).

## 1. Setting and conventions

### 1.1 The extended oscillator algebra

$V=V_{\bar0}\oplus V_{\bar1}$ with $V_{\bar1}=\langle a_1^+,a_1^-\rangle$ and
$V_{\bar0}=\langle b_j^+,b_j^-\rangle_{j=1}^n$. $A=V\oplus\mathbb CK\oplus\mathbb C\kappa$ with
$K$ even central and $\kappa$ odd central. Since $\kappa$ is central, $[\kappa,\kappa]=0$, so in
$\mathcal U(A)$
$$\kappa^2=\tfrac12[\kappa,\kappa]=0 .\tag{1.1}$$
We work in $\mathcal U(A)/(K-1)$ throughout. The brackets are
$$\{a_1^-,a_1^+\}=1,\qquad [b_k^-,b_l^+]=\delta_{kl},\qquad
[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\,\kappa ,\tag{1.2}$$
all other brackets of generators being zero. We write $A_{gb}$ for this Lie superalgebra and $A_0$
for the undeformed one ($\mathrm{gb}=0$).

**Parity of the parameters.** The bracket $[b_j^s,a_1^\sigma]$ has parity $0+1=1$ and $\kappa$ has
parity $1$; therefore the coefficients $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}$ are ordinary (even) complex scalars.
The "parity 1" statement in `C_inhomogeneous_definition.md` is read as the statement that the
deformation *cochain* $\gamma_{gb}$ is odd (it multiplies the odd element $\kappa$), which is exactly
what forces the trivialising map $f$ in `C_coboundary_definition.md` to be odd. (If one insisted on
Grassmann-odd $\mathrm{gb}$'s, one would have to drop $\kappa$ and work over the Grassmann algebra
generated by the $\mathrm{gb}$'s; the first-order analysis below is unchanged, see Remark 7.3.)

### 1.2 The Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak g$ inside $\mathcal U(A)$

$\mathfrak g=C(n+1)$ is the span of the quadratic elements of `Cn1_definition.md`. We use the basis
(dimension $2n^2+5n+1$)

| type | elements | count |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ | $E_{\delta_k+\delta_l}=b_k^+b_l^+$ $(k\le l)$, $E_{-\delta_k-\delta_l}=b_k^-b_l^-$ $(k\le l)$, $E_{\delta_k-\delta_l}=b_k^+b_l^-$ $(k\ne l)$, $h_k=b_k^+b_k^-+\tfrac12$ | $2n^2+n$ |
| $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ | $N=a_1^+a_1^--\tfrac12$ | $1$ |
| odd | $E_{\pm\varepsilon\pm\delta_l}=a_1^{\pm}b_l^{\pm}$ | $4n$ |

The doc's Chevalley generators are $H_1=N+h_1$, $H_k=h_{k-1}-h_k$, $H_{n+1}=-h_n$,
$E_{\pm2\delta_n}^{\rm doc}=\tfrac12 E_{\pm2\delta_n}$; e.g.
$[\tfrac12(b_n^+)^2,\tfrac12(b_n^-)^2]=-b_n^+b_n^--\tfrac12=H_{n+1}$, confirming that with the
$\pm\tfrac12$ shifts the span is an **exact** subalgebra of $\mathcal U(A_0)$ (no scalar terms in
brackets). Every element of $\mathfrak g$ is the super-symmetrisation
$\{x,y\}_s=\tfrac12\big(xy+(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}yx\big)$ of a pair $x,y\in V$; the super-symmetric square
$S^2V$ is the standard model of $\mathfrak{osp}(V)$.

Each basis element carries an $\mathfrak h$-weight in $\mathbb Z^{n+1}$ with coordinates
$(\varepsilon,\delta_1,\dots,\delta_n)$, read off from the oscillators ($a_1^\pm\mapsto\pm\varepsilon$,
$b_k^\pm\mapsto\pm\delta_k$). The $\varepsilon$-coordinate is the eigenvalue of $\mathrm{ad}_N$:
$$\mathrm{ad}_N=0\ \text{on}\ \mathfrak g_{\bar0},\qquad \mathrm{ad}_N=\pm1\ \text{on}\ E_{\pm\varepsilon\pm\delta_l}. \tag{1.3}$$

### 1.3 The deformed bracket and the cochain complex

Let $Z_1,\dots,Z_d$ be the basis above, written with the *same* oscillator words in
$\mathcal U(A_{gb})$. Computing $[Z_i,Z_j]$ in $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$ and normal-ordering gives
(Lemma 2.1)
$$[Z_i,Z_j]=\sum_k c_{ij}^k Z_k+\kappa\Big(\sum_k\gamma_{ij}^kZ_k+\gamma^0_{ij}\Big),\tag{1.4}$$
with $c_{ij}^k$ the undeformed structure constants and $\gamma_{ij}^k,\gamma_{ij}^0$ linear in
$\mathrm{gb}$. We call $\gamma_{gb}(Z_i,Z_j)=\sum_k\gamma^k_{ij}Z_k$ the **$\mathfrak g$-valued
part** and $\gamma^0_{ij}$ the **scalar part**. The doc's $L=\mathfrak g\oplus\kappa\mathfrak g$ with
$[X,Y]_\gamma=[X,Y]_0+\kappa\gamma(X,Y)$, $[X,\kappa Y]=(-1)^{p(X)}\kappa[X,Y]$,
$[\kappa X,\kappa Y]=0$ is then a Lie superalgebra iff $\gamma$ satisfies the $\kappa$-linear part of
the super-Jacobi identity, which reads
$$\begin{aligned}(d\gamma)(X,Y,Z)=\;&\gamma(X,[Y,Z])+(-1)^{p_X}[X,\gamma(Y,Z)]-\gamma([X,Y],Z)-[\gamma(X,Y),Z]\\
&-(-1)^{p_Xp_Y}\gamma(Y,[X,Z])-(-1)^{p_Xp_Y+p_Y}[Y,\gamma(X,Z)]=0 .\end{aligned}\tag{1.5}$$
An odd linear map $f$ corresponds to the change of basis $X\mapsto X+\kappa f(X)$ of $L$, and a direct
expansion (using $X\kappa=(-1)^{p_X}\kappa X$) gives precisely the doc's formula
$$(\delta f)(X,Y)=(-1)^{p(X)}[X,f(Y)]-(-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)}[Y,f(X)]-f([X,Y]).\tag{1.6}$$
One checks $d\circ\delta=0$ (verified exactly in the artifacts, block by block). Odd 2-cochains are
super-skew: $\gamma(Y,X)=-(-1)^{p_Xp_Y}\gamma(X,Y)$; in particular $\gamma(X,X)$ may be non-zero for
odd $X$ (and indeed $\gamma_{gb}(E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1},E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})=2\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1}$).

## 2. Structure of the deformed brackets

**Lemma 2.1.** For $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$ (quadratic in $V$), $[X,Y]$ computed in $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$ has
the form (1.4). Moreover $c^k_{ij}$ is independent of $\mathrm{gb}$ and $\gamma^k_{ij},\gamma^0_{ij}$ are
homogeneous of degree one in $\mathrm{gb}$.

*Proof.* The super-commutator of two quadratic monomials expands by the derivation rule into terms
(generator bracket)$\times$(quadratic monomial). Every generator bracket (1.2) is a scalar or
$\mathrm{gb}\cdot\kappa$. Re-normal-ordering a quadratic monomial again produces only scalars or
$\mathrm{gb}\,\kappa$. Hence the result is (quadratic + scalar) $+\ \kappa\,$(quadratic + scalar), where
terms with two $\mathrm{gb}$'s carry $\kappa^2=0$. Normal-ordered quadratic monomials are, up to scalars,
the basis elements $Z_k$ (Section 1.2), which gives (1.4). Setting $\mathrm{gb}=0$ shows that the
$\kappa$-free part is the undeformed bracket, and we verified $c^0_{ij}=0$ (exact subalgebra). $\square$

**Lemma 2.2.** $\gamma_{gb}$ (the $\mathfrak g$-valued part) is an odd 2-cocycle: $d\gamma_{gb}=0$.
The scalar part $\gamma^0$ is an odd 2-cocycle with values in the trivial module.

*Proof.* $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$ is associative, so the super-Jacobi identity holds for the elements
$Z_i$; its $\kappa$-coefficient is $d(\gamma_{gb}+\gamma^0)=0$ in the module $\Pi(\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C)$,
and $\mathfrak g\oplus\mathbb C$ is a direct sum of $\mathfrak g$-modules (scalars are central and act
trivially), so each component is a cocycle separately. $\square$

## 3. Proof A: the oscillator change of variables (constructive)

**Lemma 3.1 (the deformation of $A$ is trivial).** Put
$$u^\sigma:=\sum_{j=1}^n\big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}\,b_j^+-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}\,b_j^-\big)\in V_{\bar0},\qquad
\tilde a_1^\sigma:=a_1^\sigma+\kappa\,u^\sigma\quad(\sigma=\pm).\tag{3.1}$$
Then in $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$:
$$[b_j^s,\tilde a_1^\sigma]=0,\qquad \{\tilde a_1^-,\tilde a_1^+\}=1,\qquad(\tilde a_1^\pm)^2=0 .$$
Hence $a_1^\sigma\mapsto\tilde a_1^\sigma$, $b\mapsto b$, $\kappa\mapsto\kappa$ defines an isomorphism
$\Psi:\mathcal U(A_0)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal U(A_{gb})$.

*Proof.* $\kappa$ commutes with the even $b_j^s$, so
$[b_j^s,\kappa u^\sigma]=\kappa[b_j^s,u^\sigma]$, and from $[b_j^-,b_j^+]=1$, $[b_j^+,b_j^-]=-1$ we get
$[b_j^s,u^\sigma]=\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}$; thus $[b_j^s,\tilde a_1^\sigma]=-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa+\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}\kappa=0$.
Next, $\{a_1^-,\kappa u^+\}=a_1^-\kappa u^++\kappa u^+a_1^-=-\kappa(a_1^-u^+-u^+a_1^-)=-\kappa[a_1^-,u^+]$,
and $[a_1^-,u^+]\in\mathbb C\kappa$, so this vanishes by (1.1); likewise
$\{\kappa u^-,a_1^+\}=0$ and $\{\kappa u^-,\kappa u^+\}=\kappa^2(\dots)=0$. Hence
$\{\tilde a_1^-,\tilde a_1^+\}=\{a_1^-,a_1^+\}=1$, and the same computation gives
$(\tilde a_1^\pm)^2=0$. $\Psi$ is a homomorphism because the images satisfy the defining relations of
$A_0$, and bijective because it is unipotent ($\Psi-\mathrm{id}$ raises the $\kappa$-degree). $\square$

**Theorem 3.2 (explicit trivialisation).** Let $\tilde Z_i:=\Psi(Z_i)$ (the same oscillator words with
$a_1^\sigma$ replaced by $\tilde a_1^\sigma$). Then $\tilde Z_i=Z_i+\kappa\,g(Z_i)$ with $g(Z_i)$
quadratic, i.e. $g(Z_i)=g'(Z_i)+g''(Z_i)$, $g'(Z_i)\in\mathfrak g$, $g''(Z_i)\in\mathbb C$. Setting
$$f:=-g'\ :\ \mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\qquad\text{and}\qquad f'':=-g''\ :\ \mathfrak g\to\mathbb C,$$
we have, for all $i,j$,
$$\gamma_{gb}(Z_i,Z_j)=(\delta f)(Z_i,Z_j),\qquad \gamma^0_{ij}=-f''([Z_i,Z_j]).\tag{3.2}$$
In particular $f$ is odd and $\gamma_{gb}=\delta f$ **exactly** as $\mathfrak g$-valued cochains.

*Proof.* Since $\Psi$ is an isomorphism, the $\tilde Z_i$ satisfy the undeformed relations
$[\tilde Z_i,\tilde Z_j]=\sum_kc^k_{ij}\tilde Z_k$ in $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$. Expanding the left side with
(1.4), $[Z_i,\kappa g(Z_j)]=(-1)^{p_i}\kappa[Z_i,g(Z_j)]$, $[\kappa g(Z_i),Z_j]=\kappa[g(Z_i),Z_j]$ and
(1.1), and the right side as $\sum_kc^k_{ij}Z_k+\kappa\,g([Z_i,Z_j])$, the $\kappa$-coefficients give
$$\gamma_{gb}(Z_i,Z_j)+\gamma^0_{ij}=-\Big[(-1)^{p_i}[Z_i,g(Z_j)]-(-1)^{(p_i+1)p_j}[Z_j,g(Z_i)]-g([Z_i,Z_j])\Big].$$
Inside the $\kappa$-coefficient only undeformed brackets survive, and $[Z,g''(\cdot)]=0$ for scalars.
Taking $\mathfrak g$-components yields $\gamma_{gb}=\delta(-g')=\delta f$; taking scalar components
yields $\gamma^0_{ij}=g''([Z_i,Z_j])=-f''([Z_i,Z_j])$. $f$ is odd because $\kappa g(Z_i)$ has the parity
of $Z_i$ and $\kappa$ is odd. $\square$

**Explicit form of $f$.** Writing $\{x,y\}_s$ for the super-symmetrised product and using
$u^\sigma b_l^s=\{u^\sigma,b_l^s\}_s+\tfrac12[u^\sigma,b_l^s]$:
$$\boxed{\;\begin{aligned}
f(X)&=0 &&X\in\mathfrak{sp}(2n),\\
f(a_1^\sigma b_l^s)&=-\{u^\sigma,b_l^s\}_s
=-\sum_{j}\Big(\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,-}\{b_j^+,b_l^s\}_s-\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,+}\{b_j^-,b_l^s\}_s\Big),\\
f(N)&=a_1^+u^--a_1^-u^+
=\sum_j\Big(\mathrm{gb}_{-,j,-}E_{\varepsilon+\delta_j}-\mathrm{gb}_{-,j,+}E_{\varepsilon-\delta_j}
-\mathrm{gb}_{+,j,-}E_{-\varepsilon+\delta_j}+\mathrm{gb}_{+,j,+}E_{-\varepsilon-\delta_j}\Big),
\end{aligned}}\tag{3.3}$$
and $f''(a_1^\sigma b_l^s)=-\tfrac12[u^\sigma,b_l^s]=\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,l,s}$, $f''=0$ on
$\mathfrak g_{\bar 0}$. So $f$ maps $\mathfrak{so}(2)\to\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$ and
$\mathfrak g_{\bar1}\to\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$, and is visibly odd. For $n=1$ (labels as in the artifacts):

```
f(N)         = + gb_{a1^+,b1^+} E_{-e-d1} - gb_{a1^+,b1^-} E_{-e+d1} - gb_{a1^-,b1^+} E_{e-d1} + gb_{a1^-,b1^-} E_{e+d1}
f(E_{e+d1})  = + gb_{a1^+,b1^+} h1       - gb_{a1^+,b1^-} E_{2d1}
f(E_{e-d1})  = + gb_{a1^+,b1^+} E_{-2d1} - gb_{a1^+,b1^-} h1
f(E_{-e+d1}) = + gb_{a1^-,b1^+} h1       - gb_{a1^-,b1^-} E_{2d1}
f(E_{-e-d1}) = + gb_{a1^-,b1^+} E_{-2d1} - gb_{a1^-,b1^-} h1
f(E_{±2d1}) = f(h1) = 0
```

Since Theorem 3.2 holds for every $n$ symbolically (no property of $n$ was used), Proof A is
complete for all $n\ge1$.

## 4. Proof B: vanishing of the odd cohomology

**Lemma 4.1 (parity = $N$-degree mod 2).** By (1.3), $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_{k\in\{-1,0,1\}}\mathfrak g^{(k)}$
with $\mathfrak g^{(0)}=\mathfrak g_{\bar0}$ and $\mathfrak g^{(\pm1)}\subset\mathfrak g_{\bar1}$; hence
$p(X)\equiv\deg_N(X)\pmod 2$ for homogeneous $X$.

**Corollary 4.2.** No non-zero odd cochain has $N$-weight $0$. Indeed, a $k$-cochain component
sending $Z_{i_1}\otimes\dots\otimes Z_{i_k}$ to $Z_m$ has $N$-weight $w=\deg_N Z_m-\sum_r\deg_N Z_{i_r}$,
and for an odd cochain $p_m=\sum_rp_{i_r}+1$, so $w\equiv1\pmod 2$, $w\neq0$.

**Proposition 4.3 (explicit homotopy).** Let $\gamma$ be an odd 2-cocycle, decomposed into
$N$-weight components $\gamma=\sum_w\gamma^{(w)}$ (each component is again a cocycle since $d$
commutes with $\mathrm{ad}_N$). Define the odd map
$$f_N(Y):=\sum_{w}\frac1w\,\gamma^{(w)}(N,Y),\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad
f_N(Z_i)^m=\frac{\gamma(N,Z_i)^m}{\deg_NZ_m-\deg_NZ_i}.\tag{4.1}$$
Then $\gamma=\delta f_N$.

*Proof.* Fix a weight component and write $\gamma$ for it, $w\ne0$ by Corollary 4.2. Put $X=N$ (even)
in (1.5): signs become $(-1)^{p_N}=1$, $(-1)^{p_Np_Y}=1$, $(-1)^{p_Np_Y+p_Y}=(-1)^{p_Y}$, so
$$\gamma(N,[Y,Z])+[N,\gamma(Y,Z)]-\gamma([N,Y],Z)-[\gamma(N,Y),Z]-\gamma(Y,[N,Z])-(-1)^{p_Y}[Y,\gamma(N,Z)]=0 .$$
For $Y,Z$ of $N$-degrees $n_Y,n_Z$: $[N,\gamma(Y,Z)]=(w+n_Y+n_Z)\gamma(Y,Z)$,
$\gamma([N,Y],Z)=n_Y\gamma(Y,Z)$, $\gamma(Y,[N,Z])=n_Z\gamma(Y,Z)$. Also
$[\gamma(N,Y),Z]=-(-1)^{(p_Y+1)p_Z}[Z,\gamma(N,Y)]$ since $\gamma(N,Y)$ has parity $p_Y+1$. Hence
$$w\,\gamma(Y,Z)=(-1)^{p_Y}[Y,\gamma(N,Z)]-(-1)^{(p_Y+1)p_Z}[Z,\gamma(N,Y)]-\gamma(N,[Y,Z])
=w\,(\delta f_N)(Y,Z)$$
by (1.6) with $f_N=\tfrac1w\gamma(N,\cdot)$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.4.** $H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$ for all $n\ge1$; in fact the same argument gives
$H^k(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$ for all $k$ (Cartan's formula $\mathcal L_N=d\iota_N+\iota_Nd$ with
$\mathcal L_N$ invertible on every odd cochain). Likewise $H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathbb C)=0$: odd central
extensions of $C(n+1)$ are trivial.

*Proof.* Proposition 4.3 is the degree-2 case; for the trivial module the same computation with
$\gamma^0$ gives $\gamma^0=-f_N''\circ[\cdot,\cdot]$ with $f''_N(Y)=\gamma^0(N,Y)/(-n_Y)$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.5 (Proof B of the Theorem).** By Lemma 2.2, $\gamma_{gb}$ is an odd 2-cocycle for every
value of $\mathrm{gb}$; by Theorem 4.4 it is a coboundary, with the explicit primitive (4.1). For $n=1$:

```
f_N(E_{2d1})  = -2 gb_{a1^+,b1^+} E_{-e+d1} - 2 gb_{a1^-,b1^+} E_{e+d1}
f_N(E_{-2d1}) = -2 gb_{a1^+,b1^-} E_{-e-d1} - 2 gb_{a1^-,b1^-} E_{e-d1}
f_N(h1)       = - gb_{a1^+,b1^+} E_{-e-d1} - gb_{a1^+,b1^-} E_{-e+d1} - gb_{a1^-,b1^+} E_{e-d1} - gb_{a1^-,b1^-} E_{e+d1}
f_N(E_{e+d1}) = + gb_{a1^+,b1^+} N ,   f_N(E_{e-d1}) = + gb_{a1^+,b1^-} N
f_N(E_{-e+d1})= - gb_{a1^-,b1^+} N ,   f_N(E_{-e-d1})= - gb_{a1^-,b1^-} N ,   f_N(N) = 0
```

$f$ (3.3) and $f_N$ (4.1) differ by an odd 1-cocycle, i.e. an odd derivation; see Section 4.6.

**4.6 Non-uniqueness of $f$.** $\ker\delta$ on odd 1-cochains consists of the odd derivations of
$\mathfrak g$. By Corollary 4.2 applied in degree 1, $H^1(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$, so every odd
derivation is inner: $\ker\delta=\{\mathrm{ad}_Z\mid Z\in\mathfrak g_{\bar1}\}$, of dimension $4n$
($\mathfrak g$ has trivial centre). Hence the set of all trivialising maps is the affine space
$f+\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak g_{\bar1})$ of dimension $4n$. (Verified: nullity of the linear system
$=4n$ and the kernel equals the span of the $\mathrm{ad}_Z$ for $n=1,2,3$.)

## 5. Handling of the "adjoint representation / up to scalar" condition

`C_coboundary_definition.md` requires $\gamma=\delta f$ "as an adjoint representation … up to scalar".
This clause is exactly what is needed, for the following reason.

1. **Where scalars come from.** In $\mathcal U(A_0)$ the odd generators satisfy $a_1^\sigma b_l^s=b_l^sa_1^\sigma$,
   so their realisation is unambiguous. In $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$ the two orderings differ by
   $[a_1^\sigma,b_l^s]=\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,l,s}\kappa$, a $\kappa$-scalar. Thus the lift of $\mathfrak g$ into
   $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$ is only canonical modulo $\kappa\,\mathbb C$, and correspondingly the brackets (1.4)
   acquire the scalar part $\gamma^0$. For the doc's ordering ($a_1^\sigma b_l^s$, fermion first) $\gamma^0\ne0$;
   e.g. for $n=1$, $\gamma^0(N,E_{\varepsilon+\delta_1})=-\tfrac12\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$ and
   $\gamma^0(E_{2\delta_1},E_{\varepsilon-\delta_1})=\mathrm{gb}_{+,1,+}$.
2. **Why they are invisible in the adjoint representation.** $\kappa c$ ($c\in\mathbb C$) is central in
   $\mathcal U(A_{gb})$ and in $L$, so $\mathrm{ad}(\kappa c)=0$. The adjoint representation of $L$ (on itself,
   on $A$, or on $\mathcal U(A)$) sees only the $\mathfrak g$-valued part $\gamma_{gb}$. "Equal as an adjoint
   representation" therefore means $\gamma_{gb}=\delta f$ modulo $\kappa\mathbb C$, i.e. equality of the
   $\mathfrak g$-valued parts. Theorem 3.2 gives this equality **exactly** (zero residual, not merely
   modulo scalars), and additionally identifies the scalar residual as the trivial odd central-extension
   cocycle $\gamma^0=-f''\circ[\cdot,\cdot]=\delta_{\rm triv}f''$, i.e. it is removed by the central shift
   $Z_i\mapsto Z_i+\kappa f''(Z_i)$, which acts trivially in the adjoint representation.
3. **Ordering independence.** With the super-symmetrised realisation
   $E_{\pm\varepsilon\pm\delta_l}=\tfrac12(a_1^\sigma b_l^s+b_l^sa_1^\sigma)$ the scalar parts vanish identically
   ($\gamma^0=0$, $f''=0$) while the $\mathfrak g$-valued $\gamma_{gb}$ is unchanged (verified for
   $n=1,2,3$). So the "up to scalar" clause only compensates for the ordering convention; the mathematical
   content (the class of $\gamma_{gb}$ in $H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)$) does not depend on it.
4. **The other reading.** If "up to scalar" is read as $\gamma=c\,\delta f$ for some $c\in\mathbb C^\times$,
   nothing changes: $\delta$ is linear, so $\gamma=\delta(c f)$.

In all readings the triviality question is the vanishing of $[\gamma_{gb}]\in H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)$,
which holds for every $\mathrm{gb}$.

## 7. Remarks

**7.1 Why the answer is "always trivial".** The deformation lives entirely in the oscillator algebra
$A$, where it is a modification of the pre-oscillator form $(\cdot|\cdot)$ by mixed even–odd terms. Because
the even block $\omega=(\cdot|\cdot)|_{V_{\bar0}}$ is non-degenerate, any mixed term can be absorbed by
shifting the odd generators by $\kappa\,\omega^{-1}(\text{mixed row})$; this is Lemma 3.1 and it is the
whole mechanism. The cohomological argument (Section 4) shows the same conclusion is forced by the
$\mathbb Z$-grading of $\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ by $\mathfrak{so}(2)$, which is the structural feature that
distinguishes $C(n+1)$ from $B(0,n)=\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)$ (no such grading there).

**7.2 Contrast with even deformations.** Nothing here says $H^2(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g)=0$ for *even*
cochains; the argument of Section 4 uses that the cochain is odd. The question posed concerns the
inhomogeneous ($\kappa$-odd) deformation only.

**7.3 Grassmann-odd parameters.** If the $\mathrm{gb}$'s are taken as anticommuting parameters and
$\kappa$ is dropped, the deformation becomes a family over the Grassmann algebra
$\Lambda=\Lambda[\mathrm{gb}_\alpha]$; its first-order part is $\sum_\alpha\mathrm{gb}_\alpha\gamma_\alpha$ with
$\gamma_\alpha$ the odd cocycles stored in the artifacts (one per $\alpha$), each trivial by Theorem 4.4,
and the change of variables $a_1^\sigma\mapsto a_1^\sigma+u^\sigma$ still trivialises the family (the
quadratic correction $[u^-,u^+]\in\Lambda_{\bar0}$ is a nilpotent even scalar absorbed by rescaling $a_1^+$).
The conclusion is unchanged.

## 8. Conclusion

The inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma_{gb}$ of $C(n+1)=\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ is a coboundary
$\delta f$ for **every** value of the $4n$ parameters $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma,j,s}$, with the explicit odd map
(3.3) (or, equivalently, (4.1)); the trivialising map is unique up to the $4n$-dimensional space of odd
inner derivations. The "necessary and sufficient condition" on $\mathrm{gb}$ is therefore the empty
condition, and no non-zero configuration yields a non-trivial deformation. The scalar ($\kappa\mathbb C$)
residue that appears with the fermion-first ordering of the odd generators is the trivial odd central
extension $-f''\circ[\cdot,\cdot]$, vanishes for the symmetrised ordering, and is exactly what the
"adjoint representation / up to scalar" clause of the coboundary definition discards. Both proofs and the
vanishing $H^2(\mathfrak g,\Pi\mathfrak g)=0$ are confirmed by exact computation for $n=1,2,3$.
