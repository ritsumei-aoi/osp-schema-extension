## Executive Summary

This report establishes the necessary and sufficient conditions for the triviality of the **inhomogeneous deformation** $\gamma_{gb}$ of the Lie superalgebra $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$. 

### Main Theorem
> **Theorem**: Let $\mathfrak{g} = C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$ for $n \ge 1$. The inhomogeneous deformation $\gamma_{gb}$ parameterized by the $4n$ fermionic-bosonic parameters $\{ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} \mid \sigma \in \{+,-\}, k \in \{1, \ldots, n\}, s \in \{+,-\} \}$ is **trivial** (i.e., a coboundary $\gamma_{gb} = \delta f$ for an odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$) **if and only if** all deformation parameters vanish:
> $$ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} = 0 \quad \forall \sigma \in \{+,-\}, \, 1 \le k \le n, \, s \in \{+,-\}. $$
> Consequently, every non-zero inhomogeneous parameter configuration defines a **cohomologically non-trivial 2-cocycle** representing a non-trivial deformation of $C(n+1)$.

## 1. Algebraic Structure of $C(n+1) = \mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)$

As specified in `docs/math/Cn1_definition.md`, $C(n+1)$ is realized using standard creation and annihilation operators in the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$:
- **Fermionic oscillators**: CAR pair $a_1^+, a_1^-$ with $\{a_1^-, a_1^+\} = 1$ and $(a_1^\pm)^2 = 0$.
- **Bosonic oscillators**: $ CCR pairs $b_k^+, b_k^-$ ($1 \le k \le n$) with $[b_k^-, b_l^+] = \delta_{kl}$ and $[b_k^\pm, b_l^\pm] = 0$.

### Decomposition by Parity and Dimension
- **Even Subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$**: $\mathfrak{so}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$, dimension $2n^2 + n + 1$.
  - $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ is 1-dimensional, generated by $Z = a_1^+ a_1^- - \frac{1}{2}$ (or $a_1^+ a_1^-$).
  - $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ is $(2n^2+n)$-dimensional, generated by quadratic bosonic bilinears $b_k^s b_l^{s'}$.
- **Odd Subspace $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$**: dimension $4n$, spanned by $O_{\sigma, k, s} = a_1^\sigma b_k^s$ ($\sigma \in \{+,-\}, 1 \le k \le n, s \in \{+,-\}$).
- **Superdimension**: $\dim(\mathfrak{g}) = (2n^2 + n + 1) \mid 4n$, total dimension $= 2n^2 + 5n + 1$.

## 2. Inhomogeneous Deformation $\gamma_{gb}$

Following `docs/math/C_inhomogeneous_definition.md`, the inhomogeneous deformation is defined by deformed exchange relations between fermionic and bosonic oscillators via an odd central element $\kappa$ ($p(\kappa) = 1$):
$$ [b_k^s, a_1^\sigma] = - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} \cdot \kappa \iff [a_1^\sigma, b_k^s] = \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} \cdot \kappa. $$

The deformed Lie superbracket on $\mathfrak{g}$ is:
$$ [X, Y]_\gamma = [X, Y]_0 + \kappa \cdot \gamma_{gb}(X, Y), $$
where $[X, Y]_0 = X Y - (-1)^{p(X)p(Y)} Y X$ is the standard superbracket.

### Parity Properties of $\gamma_{gb}$
Since $p(\kappa) = 1$ and the bracket preserves superdegree ($p([X, Y]_\gamma) = p(X) + p(Y) \pmod 2$), the deformation map $\gamma_{gb}: \mathfrak{g} \otimes \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ reverses parity:
$$ p(\gamma_{gb}(X, Y)) = p(X) + p(Y) + 1 \pmod 2. $$
Specifically:
- $\gamma_{gb}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}, \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$
- $\gamma_{gb}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}, \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$
- $\gamma_{gb}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}, \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$

## 3. Theoretical Handling of the "Adjoint Representation / Up to Scalar" Condition

A critical theoretical aspect specified in `docs/math/C_inhomogeneous_definition.md` and `docs/math/C_coboundary_definition.md` is that $C(n+1)$ acts via its **adjoint representation**.

### Mathematical Derivation of Scalar Equivalence
In the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{U}(A)$, any element $W \in \mathcal{U}(A)$ acts on $\mathfrak{g}$ via the adjoint action:
$$ \mathrm{ad}(W)(T) = [W, T] = W T - (-1)^{p(W)p(T)} T W. $$

If $W = \lambda \cdot \mathbf{1}$ is a scalar multiple of the identity operator $\mathbf{1}$, then for all $T \in \mathfrak{g}$:
$$ [\lambda \cdot \mathbf{1}, T] = 0. $$
Thus, scalar identity terms $\mathbb{C} \cdot \mathbf{1}$ lie in the kernel of the adjoint representation. Two elements $W_1, W_2 \in \mathcal{U}(A)$ represent the **same adjoint operator** on $\mathfrak{g}$ if and only if:
$$ W_1 - W_2 \in \mathbb{C} \cdot \mathbf{1}. $$

Therefore, when evaluating $\gamma_{gb}(X, Y)$ and $(\delta f)(X, Y)$ within $\mathcal{U}(A)$, any constant scalar term generated by normal ordering (such as $[b_k^-, b_k^+] = 1$ or $\{a_1^-, a_1^+\} = 1$) vanishes under the adjoint action. The triviality equation is evaluated strictly **modulo scalars**:
$$ \gamma_{gb}(X, Y) \equiv (\delta f)(X, Y) \pmod{\mathbb{C} \cdot \mathbf{1}}. $$

## 4. Analytical Proof of Triviality Conditions

### 4.1 Parameterization of the Coboundary Operator $\delta f$
An odd linear map $f: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{g}$ satisfies:
- $f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$, parameterised by coefficients $\phi_{E, O}$ ($E \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}, O \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$).
- $f(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) \subseteq \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$, parameterised by coefficients $\phi_{O, E}$ ($O \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}, E \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$).

The coboundary operator $\delta f$ is defined by:
$$ (\delta f)(X, Y) = (-1)^{p(X)} [X, f(Y)]_0 - (-1)^{(p(X)+1)p(Y)} [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0). $$

### 4.2 Whitehead's Lemma Reduction on $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$
Consider pair $X, Y \in \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ (purely bosonic even generators):
- $\gamma_{gb}(X, Y) = 0$ because neither $X$ nor $Y$ contains fermionic operators $a_1^\pm$.
- $(\delta f)(X, Y) = [X, f(Y)]_0 - [Y, f(X)]_0 - f([X, Y]_0) = 0$.

This states that $f|_{\mathfrak{sp}(2n)}: \mathfrak{sp}(2n) \to \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$ is a 1-cocycle for the representation of the semi-simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$ on $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}$. Since $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}} \cong \mathbb{C}^{2n} \oplus \mathbb{C}^{2n}$ is a direct sum of fundamental representations of $\mathfrak{sp}(2n)$, **Whitehead's Lemma** guarantees that:
$$ H^1(\mathfrak{sp}(2n), \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}) = 0. $$
Thus, $f|_{\mathfrak{sp}(2n)}$ is a 1-coboundary. By adjusting $f$ by an inner derivation (which leaves $\delta f$ invariant), we can set:
$$ f(X) = 0 \quad \forall X \in \mathfrak{sp}(2n). $$

### 4.3 Evaluation on $(Z, O_{\sigma, k, s})$
Now consider $X = Z = a_1^+ a_1^-$ (the $\mathfrak{so}(2)$ generator) and $Y = O_{\sigma, k, s} = a_1^\sigma b_k^s$:
- Direct computation in $\mathcal{U}(A)$ yields:
  $$ \gamma_{gb}(Z, a_1^+ b_k^s) = \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, s} \, a_1^+ a_1^- \equiv \mathrm{gb}_{+, k, s} \, Z \pmod{\mathbb{C} \cdot \mathbf{1}}. $$
  $$ \gamma_{gb}(Z, a_1^- b_k^s) = - \mathrm{gb}_{-, k, s} \, (1 - a_1^+ a_1^-) \equiv \mathrm{gb}_{-, k, s} \, Z \pmod{\mathbb{C} \cdot \mathbf{1}}. $$
- For the coboundary operator:
  $$ (\delta f)(Z, O_{\sigma, k, s}) = [Z, f(O_{\sigma, k, s})]_0 - [O_{\sigma, k, s}, f(Z)]_0 - f([Z, O_{\sigma, k, s}]_0). $$
  Notice that $f(O_{\sigma, k, s}) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}} = \mathfrak{so}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$.
  Since $Z$ is central in $\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$, $[Z, f(O_{\sigma, k, s})]_0 = 0$!
  Furthermore, $[Z, O_{\sigma, k, s}]_0 = \pm O_{\sigma, k, s}$, so $f([Z, O_{\sigma, k, s}]_0) = \pm f(O_{\sigma, k, s}) \in \mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}$.
  Therefore, the component of $(\delta f)(Z, O_{\sigma, k, s})$ along $Z$ is identically ZERO!

Equating $\gamma_{gb}(Z, O_{\sigma, k, s}) \equiv (\delta f)(Z, O_{\sigma, k, s}) \pmod{\mathbb{C} \cdot \mathbf{1}}$ along the basis generator $Z$ yields:
$$ \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} = 0 \quad \forall \sigma \in \{+,-\}, \, 1 \le k \le n, \, s \in \{+,-\}. $$

## 5. Computational Verification and Artifacts

To verify the analytical proof without relying on manual expansions, an exact symbolic solver was constructed in Python using SymPy.

### Solver Methodology
1. **Normal Ordering Algorithm**: Every generator product in $\mathcal{U}(A)$ is systematically reduced to standard normal ordering:
   $$ a_1^- a_1^+ = 1 - a_1^+ a_1^-, \quad b_k^- b_l^+ = b_l^+ b_k^- + \delta_{kl}, \quad b_k^s a_1^\sigma = a_1^\sigma b_k^s - \mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s} \kappa. $$
2. **Linear System Generation**: For every pair of basis elements $(X, Y)$, the exact expressions for $\gamma_{gb}(X, Y)$ and $(\delta f)(X, Y)$ are computed, and the coefficients of all basis generators are equated.
3. **Exact Symbolic Linear Solver**: The system of linear equations is solved over $\mathbb{C}$ for all deformation parameters $\mathrm{gb}_{\sigma, k, s}$ and linear map coefficients $\phi$.
